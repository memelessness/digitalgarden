#### Chapter 1

She was practicing her embroidery skills, running her fingers over the fabric. The stitches were rough, restless and precarious, and all that meant she had to start again. She took hold of the seam ripper, cleaving the ties between the colored thread. It was going to take her much more practice to be good enough. At least enough to the standard that’s been placed upon her. And if she can’t better her brand, how could she expect to make it in life? To maintain a home, and have a hot meal? If she can’t even solve this, then what else was there?

The ripper stabbed into her calloused finger, causing her to jolt on impact. The pain was enough to bring her back, grounding her in reality. She looked up from her embroidery hoop, listening to the women around her. It’d been a relatively quiet morning, nothing but chatter to fill the void of the tailor shop. 

“Had we gone over fabrics then?” One of the ladies quietly piped up, digging through her desk in search of something, “Like Mink? Minky?” 

“Tulle sounds more sophisticated.” Another sighed, a sort of gruffness that came with age, “More adult.”

 “She’ll find one in her own time.” The third spoke sagely, comparing the colors of  two fabrics.

“Oh, nonsense.” The second stood up with a hefty groan, stretching her arms toward the ceiling, “She’s getting older. Some day she’s going to have a house? A spouse? Maybe some kids? She’s going to need a name for all that.”

Katrine began to pout, finally deciding on the linen with the floral pattern, “And that will happen in due time. Don’t rush her, it’s the nature of things.”

“Even a temporary name would be fine.” Claude, soft spoken as always, pulled a tape measurer from her desk, her serpentine arms weaving around the ribbon-like strand, “What is it your mother calls her?”

 “Nexus.” The child finally spoke up, stretching out her legs to give her body some form of motion to battle her restlessness. She reached to her mother’s desk, placing the loom down with the frayed knots still clinging tightly. Another day, she’ll be able to get it right. 

“See? Now that’s a name!” August gave a hearty laugh, reaching an arm to help the child stand, “Nexus! That’s a name that demands to be heard! One you can roar over the mountains!”

“Her name will be her choice.” Katrine spoke pointedly with the exhaustion of having to explain something far too many times. She’d started cutting into the fabric, deft hands powered by centuries of repetition. Long ago, this became second nature.

“Well, I’m sure whatever name you pick will be bold!”

“And pretty!”

“And you.”

The child was warmed by the enthusiasm between the women that’d raised her, “Nexus is fine… for now.” It was by no means her true name. But they were right! She’d been growing up, and soon enough her name would become more important. For now, a placeholder will do just fine.

Claude reached from her desk, clasping to Nexus’ hand with a mellowed passion, “Well then, it is very lovely to meet you, Nexus.”

She couldn’t help but smile, a goofy little grin that breached her eyes and raised her ears. She enjoyed this delicate moment, a beautiful tradition she'd desperately wanted to take a part of. 

“Well then, Nexus.” August leaned against the table, looking the child in the eye as she spoke. Giving her the opportunity to feel the name in the air, “What do you plan to do with a mighty name like that?”

Nexus lingered on the thought. She’d rarely ever thought about that. To make a name of herself? Something that people would roar above the heavens? It was an exciting thought! If she could do anything in the world, she’d probably-

“She’s already practicing to take over the store.” Katrine was firm about this, lining up her working materials. Fabric cut perfectly. Thread in different colors.

August straightened, stroking her beard in her own shock and hesitation. She was debating for a moment how to approach this, knowing well of her friend’s determination, “I mean, if there was anything else she wanted to do-”

“She loves working in the store.” Katrine hadn’t looked up from her work, a hand hovering over the needles in her cushion.

August looked to the company of the room, hesitating for a minute before meeting the child’s eyes, “Does she?” Her expression eased, a sort of tenderness that only surfaced in times of importance.

“I can’t really see myself anywhere else.” This, at its core, was the truth. She’d had so many responsibilities piled onto her. Of course she’d stay at the shop! She couldn’t leave her mother alone. 

The bell of the front door rang out, returning Nexus to business. She was quick to dust herself off, running a hand through her hair to dampen some of the frizzier parts that’d lifted in her stress. And with a few dedicated steps, she’d made her way to the storefront.

A young boy, not much older than her, leaned against the counter, a bright smile across his face. The same as any other day, “Hey there!” He laughed, raising a hand in a wave, “You free to go to the creek?”

Nexus looked over her shoulder to meet her mother’s eyes, Katrine offering a nod before returning to company, “Seems like it!” She grinned, rushing behind the counter. She grabbed onto her backpack, tucking little leaflets of paper back into the bag.

“One moment!” August brought a hand to the child’s shoulder, grinning wide, “This little one has some news to share!”

“News?” Eric leaned further into the counter, cupping his face between his hands, “What’s so grand that it can’t wait?”

“She’s finally come up with a name!” The older woman couldn’t contain her excitement, ruffling the child’s hair.

“Temporarily.” Nexus spoke softly.

“... Temporarily.” August returned endearingly.

Eric’s expression softened a bit, his posture more serious. He’d known how important this was, not wanting to ruin the moment through teasing, “Well? What may I call you?”

The child looked between the two, steeling herself with a deep inhale. She opened her mouth, the sound soon dying before regaining her strength, “... Nexus.” Saying this name for the first time since choosing… It felt… wrong. She figured maybe that feeling would shake away in time. 

Eric raised a brow as she uttered her new name, pulling away from the counter, “Isn’t that-” He caught himself before saying another word, his smile returning even brighter, “It’s nice to meet you.” A simple response. It was an effort to save the moment.

“Thanks.” Her reply was soft, slinging her bag over her shoulder, “Anyway, shall we?” Her smile crept back as she stepped away from the counter

Eric nodded, looking behind the curtain to wave Katrine goodbye. He held the door open, Nexus trotting out with a newfound vigor in her step.

“You’re peppy today.” He laughed, tucking his hands into his pockets as they made their usual trek into the treeline. 

Leaves cascaded to the ground, crunching beneath each step. Each crushed leaf was a little symphony that filled the air between them. They were often quiet during this part of the journey, save for an occasional humming melody between either part. But this time, the silence was more strict, bubbling up in an excitement that bid to be hidden until they’d been far enough away from the peeping ears of their village. 

“I’ve been learning some new spells.” Nexus broke the silence, looking back with a glint in her eye, “It’s a new chapter! I call it Kitchen Magic.”

Eric whistled in response, enjoying the enthusiasm she exuded, “And what makes it so kitchen-specific?”

“I’m so glad you asked!” With the excitement of any good businessman, Nexus set her bag to the ground, leaves crunching beneath its weight as she pulled out a poorly bound notebook.

This notebook had been her own secret investment, notating her pursuit of magic behind her mother’s back. The leather was worn, rubbed raw at the spine and flaking around the edges. She flipped through its pages, an array of clippings and drawings made in the margins between scribbled nonsense.

Nexus raised her hand to the sky, reciting the incantation. She flicked her wrist with the words, pathetic embers spilling out from her fingertips. She watched them disappear as they reached her eyes, and returned to her bag, “Wait hold on. It works better with some paper or something.”

“How about we wait before setting off any fire shows?” Eric hesitated for a moment, taking hold of the book with delicate reverence to her craft, “There’s a lot of leaves here… and I wouldn’t know how to explain that to your mom.”
  
He flipped through the pages before holding the book out, “A levitation spell?” He laughed under his breath, pointing to the ground in a smooth, still motion. The leaves around them began to stir, the breeze following his direction.

This had become normal in how they’d pass their time, exchanging spells to one another in secret. But Eric was far different from Nexus. Magic was something that came so naturally to him. And it’s not that she wasn’t capable, but she had to try harder. When things were simple to Eric, she’d simply find the opportunity to practice.

Nexus took a deep breath, holding her hands out to follow his lead. The leaves swayed at first, but in her excitement, the wind began to erupt, whipping around them as the trees shook.

Eric saw it too, now and then. While Nexus would sometimes struggle in her pursuit of magical adeptness, there was something far more powerful deep down. Something that begged to be released. And had she not been so committed to practicing, it could have been enough to level battlefields.

He pressed a hand to her shoulder, breaking her focus and the leaves cascaded gently downward to the ground, “Let’s make it to the creek and you can show me your fire trick.”

Nexus looked over the creek-side, meeting her eyes as the water rippled. A sea of yellow, bright magenta filling the center. It was a look that caused her hair to stand, black curls winding across her eyelashes. 

Eric sighed, peeling his shoes from his feet, “What’re you thinking?” He rolled the legs of his pants up, exposing hooves, and strong, goatlike legs to follow.

“I don’t look like her.” She mumbled, her hair settling as the thought escaped her. She watched as her eyes shrank, the fear of such a notion bringing a shiver down her neck, “Mom she… I don’t know if she just…” She struggled for words, watching as a fish passed by her reflection. She swatted at it, watching as the ripples contorted her features.  

Eric had been all too knowing of the pressures Nexus’ family put on her shoulders. If not her mother, then her grandmother. A constant push and pull between two directions and she desperately tried to claw to another, “I’m of the belief that people aren’t simply bound to one destiny.” He spoke with confidence, stepping into the water as the chilly stream passed over his hooves.

Nexus was taken back by this, back arching as she made herself comfortable in the grass “Oh, I’m not sure the Father would like to hear that.” She teased, her fingers circling around in the water. 

“I just think life is lived through the choices we make.” He spoke sagely, guiding his feet through the water,  “How else are we to endure the Land of Milk and Honey if we’re not made to strive for something greater… I don’t believe that the Mother Above has one set destiny for us, and it lies in our hands to decide which fate is ours for the taking.” 

Nexus watched as he spoke. It was always interesting to her how someone so devout in his religion would question such a fundamentality to life. The very boy who studied under the guidance of their village’s pastor, waxing poetics about the unplanned-ness of life itself. 

She couldn’t bring herself to the same level of faith. She’d memorized the scriptures long ago, tales that blurred along the edges of her mind. Despite it all, her mother was devout. She’d often volunteer her and her daughter’s help to the church, to the community, to faith. Nexus was never too sure what had prompted such devotion, but knowing her grandmother, she’d had some sneaking suspicions.  

“I don’t want to take over for my mother.” She released as her mind calmed, picking at rocks that smoothed against ages of the creek’s gentle current, “I don’t mind working the front I guess? But… I’m not good at sewing.” 

“Well, that just takes time.” Eric offered, taking a seat beside her, “But I understand. You’re being pulled into a direction that’s so opposed to your nature.”

Nexus pouted into the grass, gently lobbing the stone back into the water. The impact broke apart her reflection, until all that was left was color, “I want to practice magic.” She muttered into her arm, watching as her reflection regained shape. Her eyes glowed softly in the shade of the trees. It was a calming look that summarized the thoughts that’d spiraled in her mind. 

“And I know I’m not good at it. I know I have trouble with it… but… I want to be able to go on adventures! Learn some more spells? Fill out my book a bit more?” She brought herself up to sit on the ground, pulling her book in her lap, “But I can’t even practice at home. Mom would be pissed.”

“Why is that?” Eric spoke softly, offering her room to air her grievances.

“I don’t know! She doesn’t talk to me about this!” She grumbled, tracing one of the runes etched into the leather of the cover, “I’m pretty sure it has something to do with Gran.”

“To be fair, she’s a scary lady.” Eric leaned back, laying down in the grass as his hooves paddled in the water, “Didn’t she gift you like… teeth or something?”

“Knucklebones.” Nexus smiled, “They’re for Scrying.”

“And have you ever scried before?”

She shrugged the thought off, watching as the breeze rustled the leaves above them, “It’s nice to have the option.”

“And that’s why you get along with her.” He laughed, nudging her with his knee, “... I thought you hated that nickname though.”

“Hate’s a bit strong.” She muttered, running her fingers over the grass, “It’s not a permanent name it’s just… familiar.” 

Nexus’ grandmother had told her before how the nickname came to be. How the women of her coven were brought to this world with an omen, and with her birth was a great storm. One that rivaled a blizzard. Sometimes she’d find comfort in finding a greater meaning to her upbringing.  

“As long as you're comfortable.” He moved a gentle hand over hers, and for a soft moment, their eyes made contact, “I would be more than happy to greet you again, when the time comes.” 

“Oh, don’t be cheesy on me.” She couldn’t help the laugh that escaped her, swatting the hand away, “... And I’d be more than happy to share, whenever I find myself.”

The quiet persisted between the two, the boy's smile transitioning from charming to mischievous with a flick of his wrist as a bauble of water splashed against Nexus’ face. She frowned deeply in response, wiping at her face to dry it. Her hair was wet, little curls clinging to her cheeks.

“You still have a fire trick to show me.” He reached over, flipping between the pages of the book before finding the spell she’d attempted to show off earlier, “Give it another try.”
