#Jwriting #Scene 


Characters:[[People/MAIN STORY RELEVENT/Aidoneus\|Aidoneus]], [[People/MAIN STORY RELEVENT/Cosmos\|Cosmos]]

"I didn't know you still smoked." Aidoneus said, by way of greeting, as he closed the door behind him. Cosmos flinched, shoulders jumping up toward her ears, her earrings clattering. 

"Don't sneak around like that," she scolded him, as the tension slowly worked its way out of her body. "It's impolite to skulk in others' homes."

"My apologies." He moved to stand next to her, leaning on the deck railing. "I'm still curious, though."

"I don't smoke," she said, putting the pipe down where she’d found it, forgotten on the smooth wooden railing. "I was just looking at it."

Most people wouldn't have been able to see the slight twinge in her jaw as she lied, but Aid did. He could lie freely, but she could not, and he knew that her teeth pressed together as she forced the pain down and away.

"That's not my flower in there. I never leave a full bowl packed." He pointed at it, casual and smiling, keeping the accusation out of his voice. 

She glared at him. "I don't smoke as a habit. And I never have smoked much, actually, only when I was with you."

"That's surprising."

"How so?"

"You just seem like the type of person who could really benefit." Aidoneus grabbed the pipe and went about packing it properly, dusting some looser pieces from the rim and using his thumb to press the material down. He made the choice not to mock her for her haphazard method, and simply fixed it with quick movements; it was his own fault, anyways. He had never made her ash or pack, when she smoked with him. "I mean, you're always so tense. You don't mind if I take a few pulls? It is my pipe, after all."

She shrugged as he took a small, leather matchbook out of his pocket and laid it on the railing in front of her. She looked away.

"I won't smoke any if you don't want me to, Cos, I know this stuff can be expensive, in some territories."

She didn't turn back. "I don't pay for it, so."

"Oh?"

"I grow it. Well-- not as a habit. I started growing it a bit ago, after you sent me your letter. I remembered you liked it, and I thought maybe I could use some of that now, so I just started some in the greenhouse. You can light it." Without looking, she slid the matchbook towards him. He didn't mock her for the way she wrapped her sentiments in orders so he wouldn't read into them, even though he thought it might be funny to do so.

He took it up and turned away from her to strike the match, holding the pipe in his right hand, cradling the bowl gently.

She didn't comment on him turning away, but held her hand out for the pipe when he turned back, streaming an exhale from his nose, match already extinguished and tossed onto the lawn for the sliver of wood to decompose.

"Pretty alright," Aidoneus said, the air of a connoisseur entering his voice as Cosmos began to pull from the pipe herself.

"Yeah, well." Cosmos shrugged, looking out over the yard, taking small pulls and small exhales in rapid succession, giving her throat a break between them.

A few puffs of smoke wafted by in the companionable silence.

"I would've just let you use the pipe, if you'd asked," Aidoneus informed her as she passed it back. He'd lit it well, to keep the burn going in case the wind kicked up, but it meant they'd have to finish it fast. He preferred less fire for a more casual smoke, usually, but he didn't want to keep striking matches around the Lady of the house. He was quick to pass it back to her, taking a few small hits, releasing them in quick puffs out of the corner of his mouth.

"Yeah, I'm sorry about that." Cosmos shuffled one of her feet against the deck. "I, um..." She chewed on her lip. "Yeah. I don't really know why I wanted to use it. Or why I started to, either. I just saw it, and..."

"It's not a big deal. Honestly, I'd let you have it, if you wanted it." He laughed, and glanced back at her.

The thought crossed his mind that he'd had the pipe for a very, very long time. He could clearly envision the pipe in his hand on a backdrop of all manner of other places; sometimes alone, sometimes with others. He was quick to light, quick to offer it to others. It felt polite. There were, of course, no written rules in society for the frequent smoker, but he had his own philosophy on the matter, and that philosophy included sharing.

"You know," he said, watching her pull in another time. "I think that's the pipe I started you on."

"Maybe that's why I wanted to use it," she said, irony dripping from her voice.

"Well, why not?" Aid asked, holding his hand out for the pipe.

"Careful, it's getting harsh," she muttered as it exchanged hands, "and I just don't know why it would. I'm not sentimental."

"Eh, I think you are. Just in your own way." He spoke around the mouthpiece. "You started growing because I sent my letter. That could be sentiment."

"You just reminded me, is all."

"I did. Because you're sentimental." He smiled at her, before turning his head to blow the smoke over the backyard. "So you grew the plants because you wanted to smoke like you used to with me. Pipe's a part of that. See pipe, want smoke, makes sense to me."

Cosmos took it out of his hand, a comfortable breach of boundaries that she knew she had permission to do. Aidoneus always felt honored when she got short with him. She was always so wound up, so proper, so tense, so determined to be exactly who she was expected to be. She expected herself to be perfect with others. But he'd made it clear to her that she could lay into him all she wanted, she was allowed to be a "bad person" when he was around, he’d still turn back up somehow.

Her idea of being a bad person wasn't, honestly, that bad.

She shot him a glare as she pulled from the pipe, but the effect was somewhat undercut by the massive fit of coughing she burst into. They'd hit the bottom of the smokable herb.

"Way to smoke it to the last," he cackled as she hacked. She offered him, in return, a very serious look between coughs. This did little to make him feel bad, and he continued to shake with laughter as he held out a hand for the pipe. "I'll clean it and repack it, if you want. My stuff, this time."

She passed it back over. The force with which she returned it to his palm produced a quiet thwack, and he up-ended the pipe to knock it on the railing to clear the ash.

"I don't miss those days." Cosmos' words struck Aid as he was in the process of leaning on the railing, tugging at the corded leather pouch around his neck to bring his small supply of prepared herb to pack into the pipe, holding the bowl in his left hand.

"Which days?" he asked, continuing his process, trying to tune out the droning feeling of rejection that was slowly running in, like the subtle warning signs of an impending flood, like rats fleeing a ship. The ash clung stubbornly to the inside of the pipe, not falling when he attempted to clear it out. He turned it again and smacked it, much harsher this time, against the railing, frowning vaguely at it. He watched for the ash this time. Nothing fell out.

"The ones where I'd sneak out to see you all the time."

"Way to word that one, Cos," Aid grumbled, hitting the pipe again, focusing. Nothing.

"Well, I mean, I was only sneaking away because things were so bad at home, and--"

The flood began to press at his attention, unwilling to go ignored as Cosmos added on, not taking the opportunity Aidoneus afforded her to rethink. "You're really not doing yourself any favors, babes." Another knock on the railing.

"What?" she snapped. "What are you talking about?"

"Listen, Cosmos, I know what you're trying to say to me." Aidoneus looked up from the bowl, turning his body to face his dear friend. "I really do. I know you're not meaning to sound the way you are. I know this is just the way we talk and the way you talk. But I'm going to need you to reconsider the "not missing the most influential time of our friendship" narrative you're spinning just for a second, and just sit with how that might sound to the guy who you literally disappeared on after your ex went genuinely insane."

Cosmos' gaze dropped from Aidoneus' face to the space between his head and his shoulder, staring off into the open space beyond, solemn. No words for a moment.

"And then, maybe, you can just figure out for yourself what I'm talking about." He finished, turning back to the pipe. The mellow feeling creeping at the edge of his mind was not, precisely, welcome to mingle with the tension. It did so without his approval.

Cosmos' silence stretched on, and she turned back to lean on the railing, casting her eyes over her backyard. Aidoneus opened his corded leather pouch and paused, staring down into the dirt near the foundation of the porch. He wondered if he had been too harsh. He wondered if she would be the one to break the silence, and he wondered if he wanted her to. He knew there was a chance she would open her mouth and make it worse, just outright say what he was trying to avoid hearing. Maybe she actually did mean that he was just an accessory to a bad time in her life, that she had no need of him, now with her life as stable as it was in the mountains under the night sky's watchful gaze. She had never had any intention of returning to him, had been content to let their history fade to nothing. She had Artemis, now, her pet human, her destined one, and the ever-doting attention of Moss, a powerful fae who could offer such great things. No need for the companionship of a liar and a whore, now. She was no longer a nervous wreck runaway in the humid night. He was no longer alluring in his forbidden nature-- she could have whatever she wanted, nobody would tell her not to see him. He wondered if he even wanted her to say otherwise, to apologize, to agree that she didn't mean it. Then he would have to make himself believe her, even if just for now, and it wouldn’t last long after the sound of her voice faded from immediate memory. 

"I… just meant that I didn't miss the circumstances." She eventually said. He felt himself unmoved by this. "I guess..." Her head dropped down, and she shook it. "I mean, I miss having the excuse to see you. I'd like to have that back. It's... um... not really ever easy to do that anymore."

He hit the pipe on the railing again, gently this time. Ash crumbled down. He had to ask. "You mean that?"

"Yeah."

"You meant that? When you said the first part?"

"It was a different thought in my head than the one I was talking about. But it was true before. Sometimes when I've gotten really... trapped feeling... I wish I could just drop everything and show up at your window again. And smoke on your balcony together. It was easy, then. It's not anymore."

He turned the pipe right-side up. The bowl was clear.

"I miss it, too," he said. "You were a really special surprise for me. It's not the same knowing you won't just appear at any time like you used to, it's just sort of... boring. I liked it. I liked you. I still do." He found his restraint, and stopped the tumble toward truth as he used his thumb to pack down a new bowl of loose herb. "And I liked to imagine you felt the same."

"Why would I run to you if I never liked you?"

"Just because I was there." The restraint fell under her question, the hurt edge to her voice. "Because I share before I think, and I can’t help myself. I don't know."

Cosmos made an acknowledging sound back, watching as he pinched herb from the pouch into the pipe. He sighed through his nose. The mellow feeling had spread out from his eyes, and there was a slight buzzing sensation in his head. He felt his body as himself, as opposed to a collection of body parts that contained him.

"This is good, by the way," He commented.

"Huh?" Cosmos' head snapped back to him.

"What you grew. It's good, I like it. It's the sort of thing I'd smoke to go to have dinner with my family. Very grounding."

"Oh. I'm glad. I'll grow more, probably, and maybe when you go home you can take some."

"Well, now you have to, because you said that.” He sniffed petulantly. “Or I’ll cry, and you’ll never forgive yourself.” He placed the pipe between them on the railing. He wasn’t sure if he even exactly wanted more. He was worried she’d find an excuse to run away if he didn’t oblige her to stay. 

Curiosity never stopped haunting Aidoneus, even in moments of emotion. It never let him rest into a silence, or leave a silence drag on when he could be learning more. It wriggled past the fresh fire blanket of reconciliation, the solace in a wall crumbling down, into the peaceful moment. 

He hadn’t spoken to Cosmos as her unguarded self in a long damn time. He’d missed out on quite a lot. The person Moss spoke about, on the rare occasion Moss wasn’t taking advantage of Aidoneus not being Cosmos specifically, sounded like a different creature than he used to know. Sure, there were familiarities, but there were differences. 

He had to know them.
