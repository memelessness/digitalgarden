Description: The one where The Hunter is introduced to the Faerielands

**

The Hunter left camp before anyone else could wake up, examining the traps they’d laid the night before as sunlight had only barely peered through the tree leaves. (This obviously needs some filler. I’m not going to pretend I know what this says yet).

He looked up, another creature staring back. A deer? A fox? No, this beast seemed to be neither, and yet both. Its form was transparent and glittering like stardust. It chirped like sweet basil, watching him, waiting. It seemed to be peering into his very soul with those brilliant green eyes.

And then, it turned around, taking a few steps before looking over its shoulder, staring back at him. It wanted him to follow it.

The Hunter looked around, checking for his campmates, but no one was there. He was alone with this decision. He stared at the beast, taking a step forward to watch its reaction.

It blinked slowly, eyes trained on him as it nodded. It began to walk, and he found himself drawn.

They walked along the autumn trees, the leaves crunching beneath The Hunter’s feet like homemade toffee, sickeningly sweet. He wondered where the beast was leading him, and why they would approach him. Most other creatures he’d stumbled upon would soon face an arrow to its side… and yet, somehow, he felt entranced by this being. 

The beast turned the corner of a formation of rocks, like a pillar that had fallen years ago, moss enveloping it. The Hunter followed, taking no notice to the mushrooms that encircled the monument.

He cowered to a bright light, arms racing to cover his eyes. His senses suddenly felt heightened, the world around him making a grating noise like iron and poorly made wine. His knees crashed to the ground as it took everything in him not to scream, not to fight back lest he attract a far more terrifying creature.

And as quick as it all started, everything overloading him had disappeared. 

The hunter carefully opened his eyes, staring back into vibrant greens. He lifted his head more. The leaves weren’t falling anymore, evergreens replacing sweet maple trees. 

“What is…” His words fell short as he looked to the beast sitting before him, its tail brushing against the soft soil as it observed him, as if it’d watched many experience the same sensations he had. It held no concern, only patience for when he is ready to walk once more.

“... Where are we?” He spoke softly, its head lazily motioning toward a stone pathway.

He ran careful fingers through his hair, looking around carefully. He looked to the sky, the sunlight glittering across pine needles. He could see the birds soaring overhead, searching for their next meal… and yet… he couldn’t hear them?

He reached to his ears, pressing them gently. Why couldn’t he-

The beast cocked its head to the side, blinking slowly, and suddenly, The Hunter could hear again. Not as violently as before, and even the birds sounded somewhat muted, as if encased in gelatin. 

The creature finally stood, and he found himself soon following, half paying attention to where he was being led. He wondered about where he was. In a different forest? But he’d camped in the same forest for nearly a week now. He’d known the flora of this area. And the fauna? Well, he’d hardly seen any, but this creature alone was enough to make him wary. 

He looked away from the beast, long enough to see the steep incline of granite, climbing higher, even stretching past the clouds. He knew for sure that there’d never been a mountain in this area. He’d come here to hunt since he was a child, he would’ve noticed a mountain that pierced the heavens.

The Hunter turned back around to follow the beast, but the creature was gone, and the path was missing. He turned behind him, and there was nothing but trees.


The creatures laughed as they poked and prodded at his legs. It took the Hunter everything in his power to fend them off, but quickly did his legs give out, his body seizing like a bitter poison in his veins. He shouted for someone to help, no longer capable of taking arms for himself, and these small animals climbed on top of him cheering over their preemptive victory, as if their success had already been determined.

“Get off.” A woman stood above him, fierce, sharp teeth brandished against the small beasts.

The biggest of them pushed his way through, approaching the woman, “My Lord.” It spoke, all but begging, “It has been much too long since my people found a feast so marvelous! Why mustn’t we take this mortal to our lair and allow our children to feast for the first time since last summer!”

She looked down to The Hunter, and his eyes locked with those comparable to evening peonies and gentle lilacs, “Because the mortal is my betrothed.” She spoke firmly, bringing her fingers to her lips as she realized she had spoken the truth. “Leave it be.” She spoke in a rich tone, like blackberries in a freshly baked pie. It was beautiful, and demanded obedience. The Hunter found himself melting in it. “If it’s a feast you want, I can provide you with a stag before the moon wanes, but killing this mortal will be the last thing you and your people ever do.” 

The little brutes dispersed from on top of The Hunter, and the woman knelt before him, inspecting his legs. She held a warm hand to him, muttering something, almost like a prayer. He blinked back at her, examining the woman before him. Her ears curved to a point, the colors in her eyes seemingly shifting despite her sitting still. She almost seemed human, but something had been off.

In a moment, he inhaled, and his body seemed to move again. Much slower, but controllable. He was able to move his mouth, wrapping around a word, but too stunned to make a sound.

“That should be better.” She spoke softly, pulling her hand away and allowing him to take his time.

“... What happened?” He managed to speak, eyes moving to where the beast had run off to.

“You’ve just been poisoned.” Her laughter was soft and sweet like vanilla, “Not as dangerous as a boggart’s kiss… though I suppose it’s a bit more than most other things.”

He sat up, holding his head and the forest stopped spinning, “Where are we?”

“You’ve stepped into my domain.”

“... And… who are you?”

She smiled, teeth pointed and ferocious looking, “My name is Cosmos, Lord of the Mountain Faeries.”

He’d heard about the faeries before, in old childhood tales. The fair folk that would cross over the veil to steal mortals to their realm… or bless the harvest. Faeries were tricksters, and not always were you lucky enough to meet a good one. 

“And what is your name?” Her voice was inviting and smooth. She extended a hand, and he watched it carefully.

The Hunter stared at the hand offered, too scared to answer but worried of what would happen if he refused a faerie. She could hurt him, or worse, his family.

Cosmos watched him carefully. She’d seen this reaction in humans before. How could she blame them? Humans, they’d learned to fear her kind. And rightfully so.

“Hey.” She spoke gently, placing a gentle hand to his chest. He looked up, meeting her eyes, and she smiled, “As long as you are here, I will never hurt you. I will never let anything hurt you.” She reached for his hand, holding them between her own, “As long as I breathe, I will never let any harm come to you.” 

The Hunter kept watching her eyes, inhaling slowly before finally letting out a word. One word. A word that, if the Faerie Lord had been lying, could harm him.

Cosmos opened her mouth, repeating the word. She knew he wasn’t lying, but something about the name felt… fickle. A given name, instead of chosen. How very like mortals, but unlike her own people. It hadn’t burned like a lie, but felt like a tinge in the back of her throat.
