** 

Moxxie stood in front of a mirror, preening as it plucked at some of the looser feathers. It observed its outfit with that old sort of calculated precision. Its eyes narrowed in irritation, smoothing out the lines in the satin fabric that demanded to bunch together at its waistband.

It let out a slow sigh, a rumbling chatter bubbling at the back of its throat. It looked to the box at its workstation, everything tied together in a neat little bow. Maybe this wasn't enough? Not after how poorly it'd reacted. Maybe it needed to give more. Maybe all these sleepless nights were really catching up to it.

... Every stubborn fiber in its body begged Moxxie not to do this. 

The most shameful part of it all was the walk. The mighty box slung over its shoulder, a bottle of wine in the other hand. It was like crawling back to an ex, begging for some sort of mercy.

Not an ex. They'd had terms. Terms that had never been discussed. Feelings never acknowledged. Moxxie was far too obstinate in its goals to even bother with anything like that again. Even if he felt... trusting. Moxxie couldn't afford to compromise at this point. 

It stood at the door for far too long, a deep shadow of guilt sinking its feet into the pavement. It took a deep, shaky breath, knocking on the door with some force.

Heavy footsteps approached    A tall figure opened the door, ducking under the entryway yet still looming over Moxxie.



"What do you want, Mox." His voice was cold. Tired. Reflective of a deep anger that had burned away to a more simple, draining exhaustion.

Moxxie opened its mouth to speak, the quietest sound escaping before slamming its mouth shut. Its eyes drifted to his shoulder, where a shirt had been tied tight to prevent the sleeve from swaying. Moxxie couldn't help but feel guilty, not because it'd happened, but it'd hurt him again. In another way that couldn't simply be fixed. 

And Tenor was patient. He'd noticed the tremble in its feathers, how they curled slightly at the edges. In the past, he'd just have stepped to the side. Offer Moxxie a drink as they both sat quietly, waiting for it to find its words.

But that wasn't the case today. As the giant stepped to the side, "I brought something for you!" Moxxie found itself blurting, the fear of the door being shut in its face overriding any sense of reason. It reached to the box over its shoulder, stretching its arms out to offer the box.

"Are you..." Tenor hesitated on his words for a moment, "Are you making me unwrap my own arm?"

"Just trust me?" Moxxie's voice was strained



He was quiet. Just for a moment. Until he let out a sigh and stepped away from the door, "Come on in. Just put it at the table."

Moxxie didn't hesitate on this invitation, scampering into Tenor's apartment to place the heavy box at the coffee table. It looked over its shoulder with a sort of smile that quickly faltered as soon as the realization hit that this wasn't quite forgiveness. Not in the way it wanted. So instead it crawled up onto the couch, its body small in comparison to the cushions as it tried to stay out of the way. 

Tenor closed the door behind him, walking purposefully to where the box was. He looked first to the neat bow at the top, motioning to it with his hand.

"Oh uh... right... let me just." Moxxie scrambled to its feet, climbing over to the table to undo the bow. It decided to just sit at the table at this point, pushing the box closer to the other.

Tenor was somewhat hesitant as he reached to the lid, taking just a peak before opening the box entirely. 

What lay in the box was far different from the arm he'd been expecting. This one was far newer. Shinier. A light was buried deep within, thin tendrils stretching and intermingling with thick cables. Along the side, a window into some of the more delicate mechanisms. Thin gears tick away, winding tight and redistributing that energy. Yet there was something very off about this... it's... "This isn't mechanical." 

Moxxie felt a drop in its chest at the acknowledgement. It knew Tenor was clever, yet it had hoped it'd taken him a bit longer to figure it out. Ideally after it'd left, "Yeah, well..." It ran its fingers against the back of its neck, pulling at the feathers in its nervousness, "You were due for an upgrade anyway." 

The genasi ran his hand over the metal. It really was new. And recently polished too. All the fingerprints that'd been left overtime simply washed away. It was a clean slate, "It's good craftsmanship."

*Of course it is! I invented the damn thing!* "I... worked in magitech a long time ago." Moxxie bit back its words, dancing around the truth, "It's safe! And it'll be safe for a long time!" *Enough to find a new mechanic.* "It shouldn't need any repairs for a while."

Tenor knew how much magic had truly meant to Moxxie. Magic wasn't simply a power, but a means to escape a deeply rooted inevitability. Moxxie, someone who held itself to such a high status, would stoop so low as to wade through garbage for a *chance* to stave off its own death, and Tenor never knew it to share before. 

"What's going on? Is this like hush money? What are you doing?" 

Moxxie floundered for a moment over its words, shrinking into itself at the accusations, "I'm just making sure you're taken care of." It raised its hands in a sort of defeat, "It was wrong of me to take your arm in the first place. That was... a real dick move. You don't deserve that."

And with that, any residual anger seemed to peter away from Tenor and he saw Moxxie for what it really was. Just a scared little being that made mistakes just like anyone. Frequently, he'd add. Moxxie was more stubborn than a mule, and yet slowly, and almost annoyingly, it still let him take purchase in its life. 

"It was a dick move." There was a slight laugh in his tone, albeit tired. He started to make his way to the couch, wriggling as well as he could to get out of his shirt.

Moxxie's mouth went dry as its eyes wandered, quickly shifting to the port that had wrapped around his shoulder. It did that. Many years ago, and yet there was still a very deep guilt that ran through its body. But that's why it was here. At least to smooth things over.

Tenor stretched out against the couch, leaning his head away from the port to grant access. Moxxie studied the curve of his neck as it sloped into familiar shapes, bunching the fabric of its pants between it's hands.

"Let's just get this over with." He spoke with a sigh, propping his head up against the arm of the couch.

There was a soft tapping as Moxxie's tail slapped against the coffee table, only quieting as it stood back up, "Yes. Right." It reached over to the new arm, slinging it over its shoulder before stretching out to the seat next to him. It set the arm down gingerly, examining where flesh met metal along Tenor's shoulder, and something in Moxxie flipped to focus on business.

"You're probably going to need to get this cleaned sometime soon." It started yammering, carefully maneuvering around the joint, "The new arm's also not going to be compatible with this, so when it runs out of juice you may want to look at making sure this is up to date."

"I'm sure we'll cross that when we get there." Tenor's voice was low, sure of himself as he held back from leaning into this sort of comfort.

Moxxie was quiet for a moment, searching for its words as it traced a careful finger against a muscle, "I... don't think that's a good idea."

The genasi shifted to the contact, looking over Moxxie, who'd simply been focused on the job, "Why not?" The words came out softer than he meant, yet with the tenderness they deserved. 

"I've got shit to do." Moxxie ran its thumbs against the metal once more, taking in just how discolored the mechanisms had become. But it was still working. Good, admirable work. It did the best with what they had back then, "You keep getting hurt, anyway. It's not worth it." It reached over to the new arm, lining it up to the port.

"We got into an argument, Mox. People argue."  Tenor braced himself for a pain that'd never come as the mechanisms aligned with each other, "What *does* fix that though is talking."

Moxxie, in its immediate reaction to this frustration, tightened one of the connections before the regret quickly sank in. It was glad though that Tenor wouldn't have to feel that again, "That's not something I'm necessarily good at." 

"Well, for one, I don't know much about your past." He was careful in this attempt to break down its walls. To give them space still to lead the conversation, "You worked with magitech? What's that about?"

Moxxie grit its teeth at the very thought, opening it's mouth in an attempt to speak, only for the words to fail to fall out. And after that silence, it took a step back, "It should work now. Tell me how that feels."

Tenor sat up with the permission, taking the motion slow as he moved his fingers. When that felt more comfortable, he stretched the arm out, fingers splayed out in front of him as his eyes followed wherever the light wandered, "It's lighter." He started, lifting the arm up to stretch out the tension he'd been holding in his shoulder, "It's... strange though."

"Give me your hand." Moxxie extended its hands out and Tenor complied without question. It looked over the metal, tracing shapes against the palm.

He'd flinched at first contact. It'd been an odd sort of sensation. He hadn't experienced a proper feeling like that since he'd lost his arm, and yet he found himself craving more as he stretched his fingers out, allowing Moxxie to do as it pleases.

Moxxie stifled a bit of a laugh, stretching its hand up the arm as gentle fingers followed the direction of the light, "Seems like it's adjusting well."
