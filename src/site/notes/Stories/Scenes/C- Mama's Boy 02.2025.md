Description: The one where Mycan's relationship to his mother is introduced 

**

Mycan observed the halls of his childhood home, taking it in as the very walls now felt foreign to him. Already, his room had been redecorated, the foyer remodeled, the gardens uprooted. All that felt familiar anymore was how unbelievably clean and sterile everything had been kept. And the reminder was all he needed to know he’d made the right decision.

“Hi Baby.” The quiet voice broke him from his trance.

“Hi Mom.” He smiled wide, wrapping his arms around the force that crashed into him, “How are you?” He spoke through quiet chuckles. She was smaller than he remembered, and even thinner than when he’d last seen her.	

“Better, now that you’re here.” She was the first to break away, reaching her hands to cup her son’s face between them, “How are you? Are you hungry? Do you want to stay for dinner?”

He’d leaned into the contact. Though he’d never admit it, he missed his mother’s doting, “I would be delighted to.”

She hummed softly, making gentle sweeps against his uniform to help straighten the wrinkles she’d made, “You got a promotion?” She was beaming as she pointed out the newest patch on his uniform.

“I have.” He couldn’t help but chuckle, helping her straighten out his lapels, “I no longer have to live in the barracks.” 

“Oh? Are you moving back then?” There was a mixture of emotion in that question. Mycan could feel it. A glimmer of joy along with the concern behind his well being. It reminded him of all she’d had to put up with because of him.

“No. I actually got a nice apartment I’ve started moving into.” He tried to give her the news gently, keeping that warm smile on his face.

She smiled, a little broken at best, but still trying to keep it together. He’d remember that she’s maybe not too much older than him in comparison, “That’s good.” 

Mycan wondered, for a brief moment, how things could’ve been different if he’d stayed. His mother would’ve been happier, sure, but how much longer would it have been until his spirit was broken? Or how many more fights before he was found dead on the streets? 

“Ah. The ruffian is back.” His uncle’s voice rumbled as they walked down the stairs, their head held much higher as a show of status.

"Good afternoon, Uncle.” Mycan bowed his head, holding his mother just a little closer, “How have you been?”

“Such kind manners from a mongrel such as yourself.” They spoke bitterly, stopping before him, “What is it you want? Is it money?”

“Harold.” His mother scoffed as he let her go, straightening himself.

“Actually,” He spoke calmly, refusing to give into their clear taunts, “I’ve been doing well for myself. I’ve got a nice place in the Westend.”

His uncle sneered, walking past him, “Surely, it’s private enough. Wouldn’t want you tarnishing this good family’s reputation.”

“I wouldn’t have it any other way.” Quietly, he’d silence his blood from boiling over, “I have a spare bedroom too, incase mom would like to visit-”

“To the Westend?” They laughed, holding their weight by their cane, “You can’t be serious! As if a Saxe would be found wandering the streets of the common folk.”

Mycan felt the sting of those words less than he used to, somehow still smiling through the bitterness, “It was just a thought.” 

It’d been too long ago that he desired his mother’s family name. Though he’d never heard much of his father, he’d used to resent the man for making a bastard out of him. It’d been long since he clipped his claws, and filed his fangs to fit into a crowd that wanted nothing to do with him. And it was in this new life he’d made for himself that he realized he didn’t need any of that.
