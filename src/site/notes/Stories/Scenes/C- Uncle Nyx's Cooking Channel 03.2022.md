Description: The one where Nyx teaches Moss how to cook

**

“Uncle Nyx? Where’d you learn how to cook?” Xe asked with a sort of tenderness and warmth, delicately pressing ravioli between xir fingers. Xe turned 14 not too long ago and xir mothers were off at another party. When xir uncle asked xem what xe wanted to do, xe said that xe just felt like cooking. So they gave the cooks the week off, with pay of course.

Nyx was busying themselves with kneading some bread, an enchanted knife chopping away at some ingredients so they didn’t have to worry about it later, “I’ve known how to cook well before the fracture, little one.” They slammed the dough hard on the counter, pulling at a corner to check its development, “And I was responsible for quite a few meals at the beginning, to earn my keep.”

Xe looked over to them, half paying attention to the pasta, “All by yourself?” Xe couldn’t imagine having to cook that much food alone! That’s why they had a staff!

“On a rotation.” They continued to knead some more. It only needed a little more time, “And I usually worked with a sous chef.” They glanced to them, smiling, “The first High Lord and his children would usually take the other days.”

“… You didn’t have cooks?” Xe’d never seen his mothers in the kitchen. As far as xe knew, no nobles of the mountain ever bothered.

Nyx frowned slightly, returning to the dough, “It was against the principles the mountain was founded on.” Their eyes narrowed as they thought about the past, “Back then, everyone had a seat at the table. Nobles, guards, peasants, even lesser Fae like me. Even a boggart would have a plate if they’d just ask.”

Xe set down the little ravioli. It wasn’t quite like how Nyx had made them before, but looked better than a lot of things xe made before. It was a sign of improvement, “I thought you were high Fae?”

They couldn’t help but laugh at that, checking the dough once more before moving it to a bowl, “These eyes of mine damned me from such a title long ago.”

“…. How are they much different from mine?”

They looked over to xem, looking over the blacks of xir eyes. A trait that Mycan’s lineage developed to worship the stars better, “You, little one, have already put more thought in it than anyone on the outside would.” They reached out to cup xir face, smile unwavering. It was a smile that not many others have seen in many years.

Xe gently swatted the hand away, laughing, “Okay then, why don’t we follow those principles anymore, if they’re so important?”

They watched xem for a quiet moment, pulling their hand back. Xe we’re getting too old for that, they’d remind themselves, walking over to the cutting board, “The other territories started making treaties through those parties your mothers go to.” They began scooping the vegetables into a pot, turning on the heat, “It would’ve been stupid for us not to go. The First Lords grandchildren all came, at a time where the four of them ruled together. None of them had been exposed to other courts before. I have, and they were smart to have invited me.”

Nyx let out a slow sigh, stirring everything together, “But they never once listened to me. For the first time, they saw servants taking care of everything, the other courts leadership being held by a High Lord, whoever was more powerful. The other courts practiced rulings like they had when Faeqelt was seasonal and solar courts.”

Xe brought over xir bowl of little pastas, wanting to keep them close to the stovetop for whenever they decided to start boiling them.

And Nyx just watched as the tomatoes began to sweat, gripping the countertop, “… If Mycan we’re still alive then… he would’ve shamed them. Would’ve cursed how they fought each other, *killed* each other for the title… He would’ve condemned these whole lands if he’d known.”

Xe was starting to feel awkward, and didn’t know what to say. It wasn’t like xe was there, “… When I’m High Lord,” xe started, Nyx whipping her head around as if xe were proposing war, “… I think I’d want to live by that. To spend my life nurturing and protecting my people.” Xir attention was on the raviolis, a hand lazily turning them over, “I think that’d be a fulfilling way to lead.”

“… And that’s what makes you better than the rest of them.”
