Description: Moss and Aidoneus got into a fight over tradition and now they're resolving it like adults. Who knew communication could be so useful!

**

Moss had been seated on the bacony, legs swinging over the snowy scene below. Xe was focused on carving into a stone, the grooves deepening in the shadows of the lanternlight.

You’re nothing like the mountain folk I’d grown to fear. 

The words rang angrily in Moss’ chest. They were the words that bubbled up in a moment of softness, very quickly becoming an argument about duty and tradition. What was that supposed to even mean? Nothing like the Mountain Folk? Moss was proud to be of the Mountain Folk! Proud to be dutybound to such honorable people!

Xe heard the door behind xem open, yet made no motion to move. Xe simply continued to xir carving, rolling the stone between xir fingers.

Aidoneus stepped outside with a practiced tenderness, sidling beside Moss. He didn’t **really** speak, watching as the small stone in xir hand slowly began to take the shape of a wolf. 

Moss only moved xir eyes, taking notice of the coat he’d worn had been a few sizes too big. Xe’d laugh, had it not been tense between them. At least he was warm. Xe was only starting to feel that familiar chill of the winter storm as the sun was beginning to fade.

“I didn’t mean to hurt you.” Aidoneus was the first to disrupt the silence, sliding his leg until it met Moss’.

“I know.” Moss spoke truthfully, but the tiredness and irritation was still heavy in xir tone.

“But I did.” Aidoenus released a sigh, clasping his hands together in his lap, “And I apologize.”

“I know.” Moss spoke over a dry laugh, a warmness flowing through xir body as a calmness washed over them. For a moment, xe felt like they’d be okay.

“I think you're wrong, though.” Aidoneus spoke suddenly, pulling out the sensitive foundation of xir very being.

Moss’ blade stopped suddenly, the scraping of which clashing against the silence they’d found between themselves, “The wound you’ve already dealt me runs far deeper than you know. I’m not sure you’re wanting to open it again so soon.”

Aidoneus sat in this tense feeling for what felt like an eternity as he struggled to find his words, “I meant to say… I think…. You put a lot of weight into your identity.” This received a glare from his company, to which he seemed to shrink, “Peace. Please. I mean no offence.”

Moss released a heavy sigh, pulling the sleeves of xir coat closer as xe looked to the stone in xir hands. The wolf had been xir mother’s banner. The very one xe’d been raised under. Xe remembered the day they’d taken the banners down, replacing them with that of Cosmos. Xe was happy for her. Still was… but there was a bittersweet wave of feeling in moving such banners into storage. A memory of xir mother left in the darkness, only to be resurfaced when people dared to remember her.

“Aidoneus.” Moss spoke quietly, such softness battling xir own frustrations, “I am really proud of my heritage. I don’t celebrate it because I’m made to.” Aidoneus kept silent, and xe took this as permission to continue, “I’m the last person left in my many-greats grandfather’s bloodline.” Xe’d never admitted this out loud before, and the words had stung in xir throat, “And… I wasn’t ready when my moms were…”

Aidoneus just leaned in, resting his head on Moss’ shoulder. Xe put the knife down, running a thumb against the grooves of the stone to ground xemself.

“I’m missing out on so many stories. And sure I have Nyx to help fill in some of the gaps but… it’s different.” Moss looked up to blink away some of the tears, “I’m missing out on all the things I didn’t have the opportunity to learn, so I lean on what I know. And I understand why we have differing views on tradition, I really do, but following tradition really just… It helps me feel connected to that side of me… and you saying I’m nothing like them-”

“That’s not what I meant.” Aidoneus gripped xir arm gently, bringing Moss to card a hand through his hair. An attempt to reassure him that they were ok, “I just… Not just you? No, that’s probably worse…” He was thinking out loud, patting a hand against Moss’ coat, “I grew up believing that Mountain People were these… vicious and greedy people that would stop at nothing to have their way. That we only ever invited them to gatherings to maintain peace… and… well, I haven’t seen anything like that in you.”

“But you saw that in my mother.”

Aidoneus shifted a bit uncomfortably at the thought. He’d remember the few times he’d seen Kettle. She was… a truly scary woman to him… His first real memory had been of her nearly killing some pickpocket over a necklace… but the more he thought about it, “I’ve realized I may have judged her a bit soon… and through a less than educated lense.” 

Moss leaned xir head against his, shoulders finally relaxing, “She would’ve loved you, you know.”

“I would’ve loved to have met her.”
