**

The days were returning to a sort of routine. Cosmos would spend the majority of her day working on her duties as the Mountain Lord. It wasn’t until well into the night that she was finally able to kick back. A brief respite from the many obligations that nagged her. And as usual, this break would start with dinner. 

Cosmos had asked Artemis what her favorite meal was before. A lot of it seemed dreadfully sad. Mostly salted meats, or berries that’d grown locally in her forest. She’d mentioned a stew her family had kept on the fire over an unbelievable amount of time. Cosmos couldn’t imagine keeping food for so long.

But still, Cosmos wanted so badly to spend her afternoon spoiling her fiancé. She’d asked the chefs to prepare a nice steak dinner, one with a side of tender carrots and potatoes, all smothered in a delicate sauce. Surely a great hunter like Artemis could appreciate such a bounty!

She could do without the extra company, though. Moss and Aidoneus were seated at the other side of the table, distant enough to have their privacy, but close enough where Cosmos’ own privacy felt violated. And yet, she couldn’t bring herself to ask them to have their dinner elsewhere. If it had been just Moss, the request would’ve been easy, but Aidoneus was only back for a short while. They’d been working on their own relationship. It would’ve felt rude of her to intrude on that. 

She reached out to place a delicate hand atop Artemis’. The contact was warm, buzzing with some unspoken tension between the two… That was, until the human pulled their hand away. A polite decline to her welcoming invitation.  

Now irritated, Cosmos leaned against the table, pushing the food around on her plate. She’d heard that humans sometimes took their time with romance, but actually experiencing it was jarring. Especially with her prophecy behind her back. They were fated! How long was Artemis wanting to wait? Would it be the rest of their life? How long would that be? Three? Four hundred years? … Now that she thought about it, that seemed pretty short. 

She looked up to Aidoneus and Moss as they muttered to each other between bites of food. Moss would spare a glance to her, offering a placating smile before returning to xir partner. It was something that made her heart sink a bit, watching xem as xir eyes lit up in the presence of Aidoneus. All xir attention focused on him, and in the conversation they shared.

And then there was xir company. He wouldn’t even look her way, even going so far as to hide his eyes behind a calculated hand placement. He was simply watching Moss, nodding and offering occasional input in the spaces xir left. He was smiling, reaching a hand out to tuck a strand of hair away from xir mouth, and they both shared a bit of laughter in such a silly moment.

Cosmos was quick to stand up, her hands striking heavy into the table as she tried to adjust her breathing. Suddenly, she was feeling very small, powerless against her own body. It was becoming too much to bear.

She looked back up to her company, all movement stilled, all eyes trained on her. Waiting, watching, wary. 

“Sorry.” Cosmos cleared her throat as she lifted her hands in practiced grace, carding her fingers through her hair as her nerves felt like they’d been set ablaze, “I’m going to… go for a walk.”

“Do you-”

“No, no. No.” She was able to regain some of her composure, returning to that strictness she’d grown comfortable in, “I can handle myself. Enjoy your dinner.” Her words were short, commanding, leaving no more room for questioning as she left the dining hall.

And as the doors behind her closed, so returned the unwinding of her sense of self. Why was she even mad? Here she was, in the halls of her home, having everything she’d ever wished for. Everything she’d dreamed of since the very day she’d visited the Well. And not only did she have her human, but her two closest friends were happy! Without needing her.

Could that be why she was upset? Because she couldn’t be with them anymore? That’d be insane. After waiting so long for Artemis, for her betrothed, to be worked up about wanting to still be with them?

Or did she just want to be them? For having such a short lifespan, Artemis sure wanted to take things slow. All of her advancements are ignored, so the next best thing was to give them space. Yet that didn’t work at all! And now they’re left in this weird stalemate between Cosmos wanting to maintain her fiance’s comfort, and Artemis refusing to step out of what is comfortable. Or… was it…

Was she so repulsive that her own fiance didn’t want to touch her? 

Cosmos mindlessly brought a hand to her neck, running her fingers along the burn she’d hid behind the collar of her shirt. Only the faintest of traces peaked out behind the fabric, she’d made sure of that before leaving her room. Was it enough to not pass Artemis’ notice? 

She’d stopped outside the door to the balcony, watching a figure as they nursed from a bottle of wine. Their wiry black hair was tied back, snow still clinging to their boots, and she could make out the faintest glow reflecting against the dark glass of the bottle.

Cosmos shook her head to regain herself, leaving her worries at the doorway “I didn’t realize you were back.

Nyx turned their body around, the hazy pink light from their eyes dusting their cheeks, “It seemed like you kids were busy.” They set the bottle onto the banister, sharp teeth poking out in an almost teasing smile, “I figured I’d wait until morning to announce myself.”

Cosmos took a few comfortable strides over, noticing the crown that dangled in their hand. A black mass of twisting vines, the intricate delicateness of the rosepetals chipping away by ages of simply existing. She’d never seen it worn before… only held on nights like this where Nyx had simply been reminiscing on better days. Were they better days? Perhaps just easier ones.

“It was just dinner.” A truth. One that, had she continued to speak, probably would’ve bit her back. But it really was nothing more. Hardly the romantic gesture she’d intended, anyway, “If I’d known, I would’ve had the chefs prepare you a plate.” 

“I take it didn’t go well.” They mused. Cosmos was offended, opening her mouth to speak only for them to continue, “You’re out here, alone, shortly after dinner’s even started. Who is it you’re mad at? Is it the little Forest boy? Would you like me to scare him off?”

“No, I-” She stopped herself. Who was she mad at exactly? That Artemis won’t take the leap into their relationship? That Aidoneus is so quick to ignore her in her own home? That Moss was so in love, eyes that’d once been so dedicated to her trained on another? Or maybe she was just mad at herself. That everything was slipping from her fingers all at once, and she doesn’t know how to pull all the strings back together.

“The weather’s nice, tonight.” Nyx was merciful to end the silence, looking up to the stars that’d shone above them. The sky was clear, the air crisp with the changing of seasons. It was peaceful. A scarce commodity these days, “Good for sorting your thoughts.”

Cosmos pressed her weight to the banister, tracing the constellations with her eyes. Surely, autumn was getting ready to end, opening the doors for a crisp winter. She could tell by the quiet stinging of the air, her breath rising in thin ribbons. She’d never liked winter before… But maybe this one will be better. Busier. Enough to keep her on her feet, at least.  

“There’s something happening in the Canyons. Haven’t narrowed it down, but I’ve got some people on it.” Nyx was back to business. Cosmos found this a welcome distraction, holding her head up with a hand as she listened.

“As for the Volcanoes,” They hesitated, waiting on Cosmos to nod before continuing, “... My informants haven’t made it back. They’ve been keeping a tighter guard, ever since…” They danced around it, trying to find the right words to describe the infraction.

“Since he kidnapped me.” Cosmos spoke up, looking over how the valley stretched out beneath them. Lush grass swelled under the bidding of the wind, as if it were breathing, “I’m not made of porcelain, Nyx. You don’t have to mince your words.”

Nyx brought their focus to their Highlord, watching her with… Sympathy? Pity? No, no that wasn’t quite it. It was something more genuine. Something raw that crawled beneath the skin. It was something of recognition. Something so intimate and profane that writhed its way into the meat of their being, where it buried deep in their chest.

Cosmos pretended not to notice, idly moving her hand as she watched the strands of magic that fell from her fingertips. They were delicate, the light of it cascading, intertwining, gently swaying downward as they disappeared. The scent of it was still all too foreign to her. A sour pitch that battled her senses. The color of it had dulled over time, too. That thought left a pit in her stomach. What did that mean for the rest of her? … Did that mean she’d also…

“I’m worried he’s planning something soon.” Nyx returned her to the moment at hand, “Valentine tends to make things more… difficult when he’s planning something.”

Cosmos let out a long, drawn out sigh, clenching her hand tightly, “He’s unpredictable.” Her voice had a forced calm to it, “But he’s patient. And if he can’t get up the Mountains himself, he’ll probably find some other way to bring me down.” She relaxed her hand, her thumb mindlessly spinning the ring on her finger, “Fortunately, there’s nothing left outside the Mountains for me… He made sure of that.” This wasn’t a complete lie. Everything she’d held dear had been in the Mountains. But there was…

There was Aidoneus. Her childhood friend. Someone she held so so dearly. The thought of him being ambushed when leaving the Mountains… That was something that brought her heartbeat to pound in her ears, an almost dizzying spell she’d inflicted upon herself in record time. But surely, Valentine had his limits. What use would he have to go after a Highlord’s son? When everyone was already so wary of him the last time… and the time before that… and that before. She’d wondered what’d been so special about him that he’d managed to get away with so much, and yet the Mountain people were the ones that faced such scrutiny. 

Nyx brought the bottle to her lips, a tenuous silence shared between the two, followed by a careful breath, “While we’re on the topic of not mincing words,” They started, confidence rolling easily off their tongue, “have you considered your own contribution to… whatever happened over dinner?”

Cosmos held her head in her hands, rubbing circles into her temples, “You know I don’t want to talk about this right now.”

“You said I could speak freely.” They spoke through light laughter, offering the bottle to Cosmos, “I’ve been around a while. Maybe I could impart some wisdom.”

She accepted the offer, a dry laugh escaping her, “Oh, sure Nyx. With you and all the lovers you’ve left in your wake.” She moved her freehand dramatically, bringing the wine to her lips and taking a rather large gulp. It’d been a good vintage. One that’d spent a whole generation in aging. It was Moss’ favorite.

Cosmos brought the bottle down to look over her company, the crown now held between both hands as their thumb ghosted over the petals. She clicked her tongue in her irritation, taking a deep inhale. That was too harsh. Gods, why was she so harsh?

“... I’m-”

“I was in love before… lost before.” Nyx started, raising the crown to their head. Cosmos had never seen them wear it before, the dark color mixing with their hair, the only semblance of its presence being what little light reflected off its surface, “And I’ve had friends. A family. People I’ve cared for. Those who’ve long passed. Died by their secrets. By my hand. By my side.” They rest their arms on the banister, their coat sliding down their shoulders, “You haven’t hurt me child. I know you only bite because you’re scared.”

“I’m not-” Her words stopped when she met Nyx’s eyes, like that of a trained predator. She felt herself grow smaller.

Nyx tilted their head to the side, strands of hair crossing the threshold they’d often keep well-curated, “I think you’re scared of starting a new relationship.” They finally got to their point, softening their gaze as they leaned forward, “Especially for your prophecy… It must be daunting.”

“Nyx, I’ve been in relationships before.” Cosmos backed away, her arms reflexively brought closer to her chest.

Nyx didn’t move, their eyes simply watching her, “And you’ve grown since then. What has changed?”

Cosmos thought hard on this, chewing the words in her mouth to gather something quickly, only for any semblance of a sentence to die soon after. She supposed she looked different. Scarred from a terrible tragedy that could have been handled better. She thought differently. Very differently. She hardly had the level of optimism the younger version of her would’ve regarded so highly.

She liked to be more in control. That’d always been something she’d sought after with how little regard her upbringing had for her, but overtime it’d become a need. Something to distract her from the faults she kept hidden, bound tightly behind the doors of her mind she’d seldom open… And that trusting others felt too much like cracking the door open, relinquishing control, only to risk more heartache.

“I don’t mind that you bite.” Nyx broke the silence, eyes scanning over the valley, “But I also know you know better. Maybe this new chapter in your life is a good opportunity to look into yourself. Analyze the things that scare you… maybe find a better way to cope. And while I haven’t met your fiance yet, I like to think they’d appreciate you putting in the work.”

Cosmos took another drink from the bottle, far more conservative than the last. Nyx’s words weighed heavy on her shoulders, dripping down her back like a raw egg, freshly cracked against her skin. They crawled down her spine, wiggling their way deeper. Afraid? No, Cosmos wasn’t that scared little girl anymore. She could hold her own. She had no reason to be afraid.

And yet, she had no other explanation for how she felt. The way she retreated into herself when confronted with how slow her relationship was progressing, how she tried to place the blame on someone, anyone else. She had no rational reason to do this.

Perhaps she could benefit from holding her tongue. It’d gotten her in trouble many times before. She didn’t see this being any different.
