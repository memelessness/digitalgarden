Description: Mary is sick from lack of magic and asks Aiden for help

**

“You look like shit.”

Mary had just stumbled onto the deck, dropping disgracefully onto a barrel as he glared at the cup of water before him. Marlow had been helping him lately in using his magic… not that he needed it. He was to be Lord of the Coast; of course he’d be a natural at this.

Yet being so far from the land had been surprisingly taxing on him. He’d always been so dependent on the magic beneath his feet, rather than the stores gifted within… that or the ocean just wouldn’t accept they were a part of his domain.

“I’m fine.” He huffed under his breath, raising a hand to the glass. The water bubbled and popped under his power before it fizzled out. It was exhausting, but Mary pushed a little more, trying to get the water to leap from the glass.

Marlow placed a gentle hand to Mary’s shoulder, snapping him out of his trance for just a moment, “Go see Aiden for a bit.” He spoke softly before standing up, taking the glass with him.

Aiden was probably the last person he wanted to see. The last time they had spoken, the madman had drawn a knife on him. What would he even be able to do anyway? Give him a bowl of soup?

Mary pinched the bridge of his nose for a moment. He was tired. Tired and irritable. Maybe a good bowl of soup and some rest would end up doing some good.

He made his way to the kitchen where Aiden had already been working away. On calm days like these, where everyone else was focused on some sort of cleaning or maintenance, Aiden was fortunate enough to spend time in the kitchen, chopping vegetables, humming his tunes, and chatting with whoever decided to visit.

Aiden had been busy leaning over a bucket, peeling away at some potatoes when he looked up, “Oh.” He spoke with a strained smile. The two of them had a mutual understanding that they hadn’t quite enjoyed each other's company… though Mary knew a good part of that was because he just couldn’t shut up, “What brings you down here.”

Mary walked up to a chair, holding his head in his hands, “Marlow told me to see you… I… haven’t been feeling… He said I look bad.”

Aiden watched him for a moment shallowly smelling the air around them before standing up, tapping the table, “Wait here. I’ll be back.” 

And he did return, holding onto a beautifully gilded dagger. Mary wondered who they’d stolen it from. It looked far too regal to be made by some random smith, or even as a simple heirloom. When Aiden set it on the table, Mary instinctively reached out.

“Don’t.” Aiden swatted their hand away, seating himself across from them, “If you attune to it I won’t help.”

“... It’s cursed?” He was even more confused about this. Why would they even bother with cursed loot of all things? That could be far more dangerous than the wrath of any noble family.

Aiden took a deep breath, hands hovering over the dagger. Mary couldn’t help but watch, black tendrils stretching out from the blade and reaching up. It reached Aiden's fingers, gripping tightly as the lines warbled and climbed further up. As they passed his wrists, they took on more of a silver appearance. It was becoming thinner, more delicate, like a well-decorated filigree.

And then a smell cut through, familiar and horrible at the same time. Like pitch and clove. It was a smell that followed him at times he thought he was alone, that smell that his father would return home with after long journeys out of home… The smell of the man that had tried to take his life when he was young.

“Are you from the desert?” He couldn’t help but speak up, breaking the other’s focus as the magic shot back into the dagger.

Aiden winced, holding his hands together as if they hurt. He looked back up to Mary, meeting their eyes as he opened his mouth to speak but stopped himself, looking back to his hands.

“I’m… sorry.” Mary ducked his head, bringing a hand to the back of his neck, “I think I should’ve just kept that to myself.”

“... How did you know?”

He looked back up, watching Aiden lean into his seat. He didn’t want to look at them… and that made Mary feel worse, “The smell.” He spoke hesitantly, “It’s not very strong, I didn’t even notice until you were casting. I.. thought you didn’t have a smell.” He’d read a lot about other creatures in the faewild. Changelings were prized for having an odorless magic. It’s what made the glamour market so lucrative.

Aiden took a deep breath, returning to pulling the magic out of the blade, “... I thought I’d gotten rid of that.” He laughed nervously, a coping mechanism to hide the pain beneath, “None of the crew’s pointed it out, anyway.”

“Well, they’re not-” Mary started, cutting himself off to better think through his words, “I’ve always had a keen sense for this sort of thing.” 

Aiden just hummed back, pulling apart the magic from the blade. As the shadowy magics were torn out, the blade began to look cleaner, the gems seemingly renewed.

“..... would… would you like to talk about it?”

Aiden glanced up, now holding a ball of silvery magic. The strings were tightly wound, controlled yet erratic at the same time, “A lot of people here have a past they’re trying to run from.” 

He offered the magic to Mary, who immediately obliged. And the magic was fulfilling. The cracked he’d begun to feel pulling at his very being felt restored, his reserves refreshing and spilling over. He’d known just how important magic was to his very being, but having been away from it for so long? Well, he was just relieved that there was someone talented in this field nearby.

Mary looked back up to Aiden, who’d already been moving around again, starting the kettle and grabbing some mugs, “... Not even Milo?” He spoke softly, seeing Aiden in a different light. Here was someone that they thought was so over their shit… and yet he was helping them.

Aiden’s hand ghosted over the countertop, tracing shaped, “It hasn’t really come up.”

“I’m a good listener.” Mary reached over to the blade, looking over its intricate details. Maybe he’d keep it? He wasn’t really trained with weapons but… well… he seems to have come into a lot of free time lately.

The kitchen was quiet for a few long minutes. Mary didn’t know whether Aiden felt ready to talk or not. He didn’t want to end up pressuring them. Before long, he had a mug of tea placed in front of him.

“I grew up in the mortal realm.” Aiden cut through the silence, holding his tea between both hands. The heat was simple. Grounding. It kept him in the present, “I spent a good amount of my life there. More than a lot of other changelings that were dropped into whichever cradle was closest. I was a late bloomer coming into my abilities.” 

He leaned into his seat, looking at the table, then the ceiling. Anywhere but meeting Mary in the eyes, “I was getting ready to find a place of my own. I had a job. A good, respectable job at the mill. I’d tried my best to even do better, learn to barter, get a pretty wife to have kids with and just enjoy life as a regular, normal person.” He took a sip from his drink, relaxing against the weight of his memories. Mary had never expected such a side from him.

“One day, I slipped into a shift. My mother screamed at me and ran me out of the house. I’d been her son for 20 years and none of it mattered when she found out I was… different. I had a few years without a home because of it. I couldn’t cope with the fact that my body can just…. change, and in wanting to ignore it the reactions just felt more explosive.”

“Eventually, I figured, “If I can’t exist in this world, then maybe wherever I came from would be more accepting.’ But the faerielands were just… Noisy. The magic was chaotic and wild, and I wasn’t yet bound to a territory. That’s when I stumbled into the Rumor Mill.”

Mary had known a little of the Rumor Mill. It was an organization within the Oasis that kept together plenty of hard-to-find intel… for a price.They often worked directly with the highlords as well, exchanging information from one land to the next.

“I made a pact with one of the ringleaders.” Aiden squirmed a bit, pulling down the collar of his shirt. Beneath lay a brand, etched into his skin long ago. It looked like it’d hurt… maybe it still did, “I was young, and naive, and all I wanted was for the magic to just… cooperate with me a bit. I wasn’t patient enough to let it pass. They told me it’d just be until I was able to support myself. They didn’t really give me any terms on how I’d get paid… and… well people like me are very valuable in… certain fields.” He danced around the subject, smoothing his shirt around his shoulder.

“I hated the work I did, but I was bound. I knew if I ran, they’d just find a way to bring me back. They knew I was putting up with it too, giving me more difficult clients… And one day, I don’t know what’d gotten into me, but after putting up with so much, I just decided to bolt. The client I’d been seeing chased me all the way to the shipyard. I ran to whichever one was leaving port and begged them to hide me. It was a lot different from now. Different ship, different crew. Marlow is the only one who stayed from then. The man really loves the ocean.”

Aiden laughed into his tea, glancing back to Mary, who’d just been listening. Quietly and with intention. He was surprised just how patient they could be. “Marlow beat up the guy, no questions asked.” He cleared his throat, setting the mug onto the table, “And for a long time I didn’t feel safe enough to go back to the desert so…” He motioned to the dagger, “I picked up a few skills. Helps everyone else out too. Now they let me get the first pick in loot so I can pick out all the magic stuff.”

Mary relaxed a bit as Aiden tried to bring back up the mood, taking a careful sip of his tea. It was a warming blend, the leaves toasted and spiced. What would’ve repulsed him moments ago somehow felt cozy, “I’m sorry that happened.”

Aiden shrugged, propping his head up, “... In the end, I wouldn’t trade it.” He sounded genuine behind the sadness, “I’ve made a family here. I’ve got many good friends, and a partner that I love and he loves me… these…” He made a motion with his free hand, motioning to his head, “Nebulous ideas of retirement, whenever it’s safe enough to settle down somewhere.”

“You’d do well in the Coast.” Mary offered, “I know there’s… well… classism and such but I’m sure it’d work out.” And when he was highlord, he wouldn’t let anything happen to his friends.

“Classism.” He spoke sarcastically, correcting himself when he noticed Mary deflating, “I… couldn’t do that to Milo.” He explained with a sigh, “Glamours aren’t really comfortable for him. Not the two-legged kind, anyway. I wouldn’t feel good condemning him to that.”

Mary cleared his throat, looking back to his tea, “... Maybe one day it’ll change.”

“... Yeah. That’d be nice.”
