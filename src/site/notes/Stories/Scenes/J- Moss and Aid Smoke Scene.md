#Jwriting #Scene 

Characters:[[People/MAIN STORY RELEVENT/Aidoneus\|Aidoneus]], [[People/MAIN STORY RELEVENT/Moss\|Moss]]


“I’m going to go ahead and guess things aren’t as great as they look on paper.” Aidoneus settled in next to Moss on the steps to the small garden on the side of the manor, bare feet on the rough bricks that made up the small deck. The garden itself was split down the middle: One side, a perfect spring evening. The other, an uncharacteristically gentle flurry of large snowflakes landing in sheets upon the frost-bitten grass. 

Moss paused. “It’s bad form of me to make it so obvious. I apologize.” 

“You don’t have to act like you’re my formal lord and host, Moss. You can act like my friend.” He produced a small pipe from his pocket, along with a small wax-lined paper tube. “I know we don’t talk all the time or anything, but I still consider you as such.” He popped the cork out of the tube and set about packing the bowl of the pipe with casual care. He waited in the silence that followed, for Moss to gather xir thoughts. 

“If I tell you the whole thing…” Moss started, and abandoned the sentence like a sinking ship. “I can’t do that.” 

“I mean, why not?” Aid shrugged. “There’s no reason for me to run back and squeal to Cosmos, or the human. Cosmos doesn’t need to hear about you suffering from me, that would be sneaky and backhanded and, frankly, more trouble than I’d like to get into. You look like you need an ear. And I look like I need a light, hey, look at that, wow…” Aid gestured the pipe at Moss, who reached up to the railing of the deck and pulled down a small box of matches that xe kept out there for lighting lanterns. Aid happily took one, struck it off the bricks they sat on, and lit the pipe, pulling in. He offered the pipe to Moss. 

“What’s in there?” Xe asked, holding it. 

“Soft, calming stuff. Good for travel, good for sleep. Don’t take too much, this beautiful body isn’t made for carrying swordsmen to their rooms. I’m more of the one being carried usually, if you understand my meaning.” Moss put xir lips to it and pulled, satisfied that it was no more than a common variety of flower and not something infused with magic. 

“Do you find it hard to fall asleep, when visiting here?” Xe asked, around the small cloud of smoke exiting xir mouth. 

“I find it hard to sleep, period.” Aid laughed. “Don’t take it as an insult to your home. My rooms are lovely and the beds are suitably gigantic and fur-covered, don’t worry. I just can’t calm down when it’s time to rest. I need a little help.” 

“Maybe I should look into some of my own,” Moss grumbled, passing the pipe back after another small pull. The taste of it was earthy, lingering at the back of xir throat and on the end of the exhales through xir nose. “I just sit there and– worry, I guess. Try to plan for when things go wrong.” 

“Like they have been?” Aid asked, the pipe in the corner of his mouth. 


“No, no, things are fine. Things are actually going well. I meant for this to be happening. I… I succeeded.” 

“You don’t look particularly like a victor.” Aid’s exhales were elegant and effortless, casting small streams of smoke into the evening air. Moss vaguely wondered how he managed that– all of Moss’ exhales sort of seemed to erupt from xir mouth like breath into cold air, directionless. 

“...Yeah, I guess I don’t.” 

“What was your goal?”

Moss faltered again. “You… alright, you really can’t tell Cosmos or Artemis about this conversation. Seriously.”

“There’s no reason for me to, Moss. We shared a pipe, that means what’s said on these steps stays between us. Let it out.” 

Moss sighed through xir nose, feeling the earthy smoke’s flavor still. “Cosmos came up here after Valentine…” Xe glanced to Aid, whose eyes had darkened with the recognition of the event. Xe took that as permission not to elaborate on the event xe was referencing. “It wasn’t easy for her. But eventually– slowly– she started to, to wake up. And this was the first time I was really getting to know her. The first time she really laughed with me… I mean, you have to imagine, she’d been somber and serious for months, and then…” 

“Like the first sprout of flowers through the melting snow?” 

“Yeah. Yeah, just like that, actually.”

“You fell for her.”

Moss laughed, a little incredulous thing. “Fell– it was more like she had had me my entire life, and I only just realized it then. And she still has me.” 

“How’s that working out?” 

“Well, I confessed to her at some point, and she told me that she just… couldn’t. Because it wasn’t her fate. Well, I obviously asked what she meant– Prophecies can be really up for interpretation, so I thought I could convince her, but her prophecy… yeah, no, very explicitly not me. It’s about a human, actually.” 

“A real punch in the stomach.” 

“Absolutely it was. I let it be for a long time, you know? No big deal, I wasn’t going to make her feel bad about her prophecy, because that’s not under her control, you know? And she really did me a favor by letting me know up front instead of humoring me, and I appreciate that. So I just left it alone. But she stopped improving after a while, and we were just stuck in this constant cycle of work and little else. So I decided, I woke up one day and I decided that I would do everything in my power to find her that fucking human. If it meant breaking this weird little time loop we’d put ourselves in, I was going to bring her a human to love. And– this is the worst part– a little piece of me thought that if I just let her have her time with the human, that maybe…” Moss paused, staring hard at the line of snow in the garden. Aid passed xem the pipe, and xe took a long pull from it. “I thought that maybe someday after the human was gone, she’d think about giving me a chance.” 

Aid nodded slowly, more an acknowledgement that he was listening than an agreement. 

Moss looked at him, confused. “...No big reaction to that?”

“I don’t see why I should. It’s kind of smart and mature, if you ask me. You’re not saying that her time with the human isn’t important or anything, you’re just aware that her time with a human would be pretty short. I don’t think that’s awful of you. I mean, you didn’t EXPECT her to immediately get with you the second the human dropped dead or anything, you were just thinking she might be able to give you a shot someday. I don’t think that’s selfish or whatever. I can see how it may SOUND bad, but it’s nuanced.” 

Moss blinked at Aidoneus. “... Huh.” 

“What?”

“No, I just really thought that when I admitted that, you’d think I was like– a terrible person or something.”

Aid upended the pipe over the edge of the deck, knocking the ashes out into the section of gravel that bordered the foundation with a series of authoritative taps. “On a certain level, ALL people are terrible. And all people are good. Black and white anything is sort of a lie, really, and a failure of a system of thinking. One sort-of-not-good thing about anybody doesn’t mean the entire individual is worth condemning. And honestly, this desire of yours isn’t even bad. I’d call it a neutral thing with some negative consequences. You have feelings and wants, and sometimes those feelings and wants are at odds with other peoples’ wants and feelings, and that like, sucks, but it’s just an aspect of existing. You’re not wrong for having them.” 

Moss nodded, haltingly. The idea would take some getting used to. 

“And hey, you don’t have to believe me if you don’t want to, but at least you know what MY perspective on the issue is, right? Which means you know I’m not judging you. Go on telling your story, babes.” He was wiping the bowl out with a little scrap of cloth now.

“Uh, right.” Moss chewed on xir lip a little, becoming suddenly aware of a feeling beginning to assert its presence behind xir eyes. It was almost fuzzy, a small fading around the edges. Xe imagined that was the contents of Aidoneus’ pipe making its way to xir brain. “I went out, and I went to the human realm. There was Artemis out there, hunting. So I led her into the valley. And I brought her near where Cosmos would be, and then I left, and let everything play out. I don’t know what I was really expecting, exactly. I didn’t know anything about Artemis at all. I didn’t know her personality, or what she liked, or if she’d ever be able to let her guard down and even LIKE Cosmos. I took a gamble.” 

“A gamble with no good outcomes for you.” 

“Yeah, but no. I mean, this IS the good outcome. This is supposed to be the good outcome of all of this. They’re getting along, they– they really do like each other. Um, when they’re… speaking. I don’t know if they even understand that yet, I know they’ve agreed not to force themselves to be in love, but it’s just a matter of time before they get comfortable enough to admit that they got lucky, and to admit that they do work together.”

“But?”

“It’s torture.” Moss leaned against the railing post, xir head colliding gently with it. “I hate watching them bond. I hate that it’s happening in my house, where I grew up, where my mothers–” xe swallowed thickly, and switched course before the tears could come. “I hate that I made it all possible. I hate– I hate myself for somehow thinking that this would be better than staying out of it. I hate myself for thinking I was so extremely smart, I hate that you have to break a bone to set it properly. And…” 

“And?”

“... this is awful.” Moss murmured. 

“Say it anyways, I love awful people.” 

Moss looked up at the stars. “I think I love Artemis too.” 

Aidoneus didn’t react, simply reaching into the leather pouch again. “What’s she like? She was quiet at dinner.” 

Moss closed xir eyes. “She’s… warm. I think. We’ve spent a lot of time together, since Cosmos is usually so busy. I tried not to like her, I really did– to just, you know, stay civil and distant, not give her any trouble. But she keeps— seeking me out, and talking to me, and I don’t have the heart to say no, not when she’s so genuine, and I know she’s rather shy, so having her ask ME for company is a big deal for her, and… I just… find myself looking forward to when she’s going to find me next. I find myself doing things around the house with the hopes that she’ll come and ask me to explain what I’m doing. I am, unfortunately, happy in her company, happier than I am in most company. When she opens up, she just– radiates this sort of simple, easy joy that’s so hard to ignore.
Xe took a shaky breath. “What am I supposed to do about that? She’s engaged to my closest friend and– dare we forget– my lord who I am bound by honor to serve. She’s more than untouchable, she’s… she may as well not exist.” 

“You know, that is horrible. Oh–no– Not horrible of YOU, mind you. The SITUATION is horrible.” 

Xir hand unclutched from xir chest, where xe had not given it permission to be. “You almost killed me, with that.” 

“My apologies, friend. What I’m gathering here, Moss, is that you’re stuck in a problem that’s going nowhere fast. How long do humans live? Like, two hundred years?” 

“I’m… not actually sure. That seems kind of low, doesn’t it?” 

“They’re supposed to be low, it’s humans. Anyways, my point is, that’s entirely too long to live with the two people you’re in love with and watch them be in love and be getting exactly zero time in the sun yourself. And, forgive me if I’m reading your soul too closely babes, but you seem to be the sort that you take on extra work so that they can enjoy their happy married life.” 

Moss chose not to share that xe had been putting in extra work already, so Cosmos could conveniently be in the manor more often, could happen upon Artemis. 

“That’s not healthy. That’s a new kind of cage and you know it. It’s why you’re so upset now.” 

“Yeah. I do know it.” 

“Yeah. Sooooo… wake up, babes, what are we gonna do about that?” Aid made a gesture with his hands, waving them back and forth a little. “Because we can’t keep doing this, obviously.” 

“What’s the other option?” Moss asked, exasperated. “What could I possibly do?” 

“You could try to find pleasant diversions,” Aid suggested, shrugging, sinking down to share the step with Moss, too and relaxed to possibly mean what Moss first assumed. Xe sought clarity. 

“Like…” 

“Going to bed with someone, yes, just like that. You don’t have to want to be with them forever, you know. You truly can just suck a dick and get gone. It’s fun, actually. It’s something to do with all that… energy. I know it’s not entirely appealing when it’s not the person you’re all hung up on, but it can do wonders for at least starting to move on, or just pass the time.” 

“I’ve never had too much of an appetite for all of that.” Moss admitted. “It’s just never been… an option for me exactly? It’s not that I have no desire, I just… I’m busy, I suppose. I wouldn’t even know where to begin finding out what I’d want.” 

Aid paused, took in the surroundings for a moment, and nodded once decisively.

“I could help.” 

“...Like–” 

“As in,” Aid interrupted, and reached a hand over to cup Moss’ cheek, turning xir head so that they were locking eyes, “I could help.” 

Moss’ body felt like it was rising and falling in the background, the pipe’s effects not quite enough to send xir mind away, but still changing how xe felt in xir body. 

“It wouldn’t have to mean anything you didn’t want it to. Just exploring. Finding out what you like, you know? I like a bit of everything, so I don’t mind taking… a bit of a tour.”

“I’m… I…” Moss’ words seemed to have made a full retreat. Aid’s hand slowly started to pull away from xir cheek, and xir hand reached up to stop it. 

Aid pulled closer, drifting in Moss’ perception. “Is that something you’d want?” 

“I think so,” Moss muttered.

“That’s not very enthusiastic, darling.” Aid matched xir quiet tone, trying to ensure that he wasn’t pressuring Moss to do anything xe wasn’t ready for. 

Moss, understanding the purpose of the question, snagged the tie of Aidoneus’ cloak with two fingers and pulled him quickly into a kiss. The hand on Moss’ cheek tensed for a moment, but adapted quickly, shifting cradle Moss’ jaw in a way that pulled xir head towards Aid’s. 

Aidoneus was the one to pull back, and they both breathed in unison for the first half second. Moss leaned in to go again, and Aid pulled back further.

“I’m flattered by your enthusiasm,” Aid soothed, “really, I am. You should come see me in my chambers tomorrow, when your head is clear.” 

Moss must’ve frowned, because then Aid continued, “Don’t think I’m not excited, darling, because I most certainly am. I just want to be sure you won’t regret what we do together. Tomorrow, then?” 

Moss nodded, trying to fix xir face. That was disappointment showing on there, wasn’t it? “I might lose my nerve by then, but I’ll try.” 

“If you lose your nerve, darling, then that just means I was right to have you wait.” Aid smiled, and Moss had to work harder to fix xir face. “Now, I’m going to smoke another bowl. You’re welcome to share it with me, or you can go inside, off to bed. Either suits me just fine, just remember that I’m not carrying you inside if you fall asleep out here. Too dainty and gorgeous.”
