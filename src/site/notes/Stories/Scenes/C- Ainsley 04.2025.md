The Collector__02.2023

“Welcome home, my Lord.”

Ainsley had stood at the doorway to the manor, bags of things tucked under ner arms and clenched in tight fists so as not to lose them, “Oh. No thank you.” Ne set the bags down delicately, “I’m just here to drop off my things. Maybe look around a bit? I’m not really sure if I’ll be living here.”

The human cocked their head to the side, watching this weird faerie poke around the living room, “Well then, if you need my assistance-”

“Oh no, you don’t have to stay.” Ne waved a hand, looking down one of the hallways, “You can… go do human stuff?” 

“I’m afraid to say I have an obligation to this household, my Lord.”

“No, don’t call me that, it's… weird.” Ne noticed something particularly shiny down the hall, quickly approaching it. Is it something cool? Something to add to the ever growing collection?

The human followed suit, taking note of the particular statuette ne’d been fascinated with. It was made of crystal, commissioned many years ago before moving between owners, “Then what would you wish for me to call you then?”

Ne looked back up, almost surprised that the human was still talking that ne hated to admit ne stopped paying attention, “Hm??”

The human stood a little more upright, a small smile of premature satisfaction gracing their face for but a moment, “May I have your name?”

Ne cocked ner head to the side, watching them for just a moment. The human could swear they could see the gears turning until, “Oh! Oh, that’s funny! You’re tricky!” Ne approached them, stretching out a hand, “You may call me Ainsley.”

The human laughed softly, shaking ner hand, “It’s a pleasure to meet you, Ainsley.”


Ainsley__ 04.2025 

Ainsley knew ne was different from others. Ne would think back, wondering when the realization truly hit. If it ever was just a sudden moment, or if somewhere deep down ne was always aware of ner off demeanor.

It’s not that ne meant to be weird! There was just a certain.. affect about nem. Others were oftentimes boring, or strange, or even overwhelming. And when there was someone ne felt like attaching nemself to, they would just… pretend? Pretend to understand why ne liked weird shaped rocks, and shiney jewelries, and things that were fashioned to look like frogs. For the longest time, even through adulthood, Ainsley just felt tolerated by those around nem.

And so ne stayed in ner study most days, flipping through pages of fable and fantasy. Falling in love with fabled heroes. Heroes that just… melded so perfectly within society. Never struggling in their day to day, always beloved by those around them.

Ainsley was stirred as the soft clatter met the table. Ne looked up to the figure that stood respectfully beside nem. Never sneaking up or offering any abrupt interruption to ner studies. It was the human. Unnamed and rarely ever seen. Free to leave yet decidedly staying. Everyday they wore finely tailored outfits. Ne had wondered if that’s all they had. Maybe they’d be more comfortable in something less stifling. But Ainsley had grown fond of their presence. They were kind. Predictable. Homely.

Ne looked down to what they’d brought. A delicate array of fruits, removed of stems and rinds. All looking colorful and clean. They rest on a beloved plate to Ainsely, one which the design reminds nen of the desert rain frogs ne’d find along the river. Ne never really thought until now that ne hadn’t seen another plate in some time. Not that it would be unwelcomed, but this particular one would never fail to make nem happy.

Ne shifted to look back up to the human again. Ainsley hadn’t thought about it before, but somewhere, somehow, this is the one person in ner life that even tried to understand nem. That saw ner problems and remedied them without expecting anything in return. That washed ner favorite plate just to bring a snack of fruits, and respected that ne hated being surprised.

“Do you know how to read?” Ainsley spoke carefully, very self conscious of scaring them off now.

And the human just smiled, folding their hands in front of them, “I do.” They spoke softly, as to not interrupt the carefully curated atmosphere of ner study.  

Ainsley scooted over on the couch to leave some space, thumbing through a stack of books beside nem, “I think you’d like this one.” Ne was careful to remove the book, offering it to the human.

“How kind of you.” They looked over the cover for a brief moment, “I have some tea on the stove, but I can read this when I come back.”  
  
“Oh, uh… You don’t have to.” Ainsley began to back track, all too used to such placating responses, “It’s a good book, but… well… I don’t know if you even like that sort of thing.” Ne felt nemself become more and more embarrassed over the situation.

The human watched patiently, as if trying to read nem. Committing whatever this is to memory. It made Ainsley become uncomfortable, and in that, they were the one to break the agonizing eye contact, placing the book down beside nem.

“I want to.” They spoke assuredly, “I’ll bring you your tea, and make a mug for myself. Then I’ll come back here to read with you.”

Ainsely felt more comfortable as they broke down their plan to nem, “Okay.” Ne responded softly, smiling into ner book. It was such a relief, having someone who can finally understand nem.
