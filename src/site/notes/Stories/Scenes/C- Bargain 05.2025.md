Characters:[[People/MAIN STORY RELEVENT/Cosmos\|Cosmos]], [[People/MAIN STORY RELEVENT/Moss\|Moss]]

Description: The one where Moss wants to make a deal.

**

Moss had perched xemself on the table, feet grazing the cobblestone floor of the greenhouse. Xe’d come here to keep Cosmos company, crown hanging low upon xir brow, the weight of responsibilities crashing down.

“Can’t fucking figure this out.” Cosmos muttered under her breath, digging through a drawer of dirt and wilted mycelium. She’d become busy with a new project lately, likely to distract herself.

Moss felt a level of guilt, despite xir own sorrow that tore xir chest apart. Not only had xe lost a family, but to her this was but a rhyme to previous heartbreak. Xe couldn’t blame her for finding a distraction. It’d become hard to find xir own.

Xe’d watch her, calloused hands grazing delicate leaves, dirt buried beneath manicured nails. And still she stood strong, a stature demanding of respect. 

“I want to make a bargain.”  
  
Cosmos seemed to perk up to those words, dusting her hands off and dipping them into a pail of water, “Must be important then.” A grin played on her face, bringing an unusual light behind her eyes. Moss rarely ever saw this side to her, “What are your terms?”

Moss shifted in xir seat, nerves getting the better of xem, “There’s… a party in a few years.”

Cosmos hummed in acknowledgement, reaching for a rag to dry her hands. Xe’d watch every movement. Determined. Methodical. 

“I don’t want to be highlord.” Xe suddenly blurted, anxiously folding xir hands in xir lap.

Cosmos set the rag down, brow furrowing in a way Moss would notice when she had trouble finding the words to say, analyzing every avenue, “I… can’t take your magic, Moss.” Which, in its essence, was a lie. Not only could she take the magic, but Moss would be more than willing to give it if she ever asked.

“No, I- No.” Xir tone became a bit more stern, “Nothing like that. I can keep the magic.” Xe loosened xir hands, tracing idle fingers against the grain of the table, “It’d just be in title.”

Cosmos was quiet for a moment, opening and closing her mouth like a fish, sorting through many thoughts before finally settling on, “Why?”

“Well,” Moss relaxed xir shoulders, looking over some of the flowers, “The responsibility’s just been a lot, lately… And with the parties coming up… Well, I just think you’d be better suited in representing the Mountains.”

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Came out quick and sharp, demanding Moss' attention. Her gaze was cold, akin to that of xir mother’s when she felt slighted.

Moss felt a lump in xir throat, a million responses circling through xir head. All of them seemed like the wrong answer… up until, “You can lie.” Xe regained some of xir composure, this answer seeming to be enough for Cosmos, “What if people were to ask me about anything? If my attitude didn’t match their expectations? I could drown out there.”

“So… you just want a figurehead?”

“Well…….. No…”

“So,” Cosmos approached, hands tucked into her pockets, “You’re asking me to take on the mantle, making decisions for the very land your ancestors bet everything on, with none of the magical benefits?”

Now that she said it out loud, this did seem like a terrible decision. What would xir mother think? What would Nyx think? Yet still, xe nodded all the same.

Cosmos closed the distance in one confident step, a firm hand placed between xir legs, noses nearly touching, “And what do I get in return?”

Right. In return. The other half of the barter. Moss had hoped maybe she’d present some sort of counter offer. Such deals were often a discussion, of course. And yet now, she stood so close, their breaths intermingling between one another. All the pressure was placed onto Moss.

“You’d have my unyielding devotion.” Xe spoke just above a whisper, all but acknowledging the tension between them in such few words.

Cosmos was still, quiet, analyzing. Moss could tell that she knew. Knew that she would have that anyway. That any time she’d beckoned xe’d run to her.

“I could take charge of the military.” Xe offered, looking away from her piercing eyes, “Train our guardsmen. They’d be loyal to you.”

This tension would happen time and time again. An unspoken rule of each other's fondness, each of them too afraid to commit, yet too stubborn to pull away. And it’s not like they’d never break the distance… it was just not something they did on a whim. Obligation was often a good excuse, yet still an excuse was needed.

And it tugged against Moss’ heart when she would do this. When she initiated such closeness without committing, herself. That if Moss moved even an inch toward her, would that mean xe’s betraying her wish in waiting for her prophecy? If she followed through, at least xe’d know it was her choice… even if xe can’t have more.

“Okay.” Her voice was warm as she backed away, not quite pulling against Moss’ unconscious attempt to bring her closer, yet slipping through xir fingers all the same, “You have a deal.”
