Artemis peeked into the room, the music flowing out in a syrupy melody. Inside, Cosmos had her leg to the bar, leaning her body forward, her body stretching outward, almost catlike in nature.

There were mirrors that lined the walls, a smooth, wooden floor differing from the rest of the manor. Like everywhere else, the room seemed clean, polished, well taken care of. And yet, it was empty, as if it was rarely ever used.

Moss had been seated on the floor, flipping through a stack of records in an effort to find the perfect music for the setting. Xe picked up the black record, looking over it as one would assess a fine whiskey. And with a satisfied nod, xe moved to the record player, delicately placing the vinyl to the platter. Cosmos leaned back on the bar, her leg reaching up toward the ceiling. She seemed to sway with the music, still focused on her stretches.

“I didn’t know you danced.” Artemis leaned against the door, watching her with curious eyes.

Cosmos looked over with a smile, lowering her leg, still focused on her routine, “It’s in a faerie’s nature to dance.” She spoke confidently, straightening out her body, “Though I have been a little stiff lately.”

“You should see her when she’s had a few drinks.” Moss piped with a cocky grin. The two of them exchanged a look, a silent conversation between them, “No tabletop is safe.” Xe decided to double down, unrelenting in xir attempts to embarrass her. 

Cosmos relaxed her body, leaning against the mirrored wall, “Don’t you have a guard to command?”

Moss waved a hand ambiguously in the air, “I can have the generals send me a report.” Xe laughed, meeting eyes with her again. After a long, terrifying, tense moment, xe decided to stand up, dusting xemself off, “Actually, there is something that needs my attention.”

Moss made xemself useful and made xir way out, passing Artemis with a friendly punch to the arm. Xir smile seemed strained. Artemis figured xe was simply just getting used to her.

“I have to dance for the (name) Festival in a few weeks.” Cosmos started as Artemis closed the door behind her. She returned to her stretches, focusing on the other leg.

“What’s that?” Artemis approached her, leaning up against the wall. She was mostly just curious, mostly wanting to come closer to learn more about her culture, but there was also something so alluring in how elegant she moved.

“It’s the start of the New Year for the Mountains.” Cosmos started, pretending to ignore the glances she was receiving, but in reality, she was just drinking up all of the attention, “We celebrate it every year. It signifies the end of The War, and the start of something new. And it starts with the dance.”

“Is it always done by the Highlord?” Artemis rest her head against the wall, smiling warmly at Cosmos. She liked hearing her explain things, and Cosmos had to explain a lot of things to her. But it was something about her authority. How she spoke so matter-of-factly. It was charming.

“Not always.” She leaned forward, her leg straightening behind her, “My predecessor, her wife would perform the dance… Though I’m sure it was because she was a lot more… musically inclined. She actually taught me a lot.” She straightened out once more, planting her foot firmly to the ground, “Though I’m pretty sure it’s one of those things that ended up being lost in translation.” She leaned closer, planting a hand to the bar between them, “From what I’ve heard of the First Lord, he didn’t really seem like the kind of person to dance in such a manner.”

Artemis hummed a soft note in response, leaning closer, “Do you think you could teach me?”

“That depends.” She took a step closer, “How flexible are you?”

“I think we need to go on a few more dates before you can ask something like that.”

Cosmos reached a hand to Artemis’s chin, pulling her head down to meet eyes. She could’ve sworn Cosmos had been a little taller.
