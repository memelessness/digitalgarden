Description: The one where Nyx comes back home

**

Nyx was home early. This usually was not how this went-- time usually ran away from her, bled through the hands of her disguise, until it had been far too long since she had seen her home. 

She wasn’t returning with blood spilling from her, thankfully. She hadn’t been forced from her position, necessarily, but when suspicion began to bleed under the edges of her roles, she had little choice other than to pack up and disappear. It was simpler to leave in the darkest night, become untraceable before anybody could think to look for you. Sometimes, when Nyx felt like taking an extra step, they’d leave a paper trail explaining away their shed persona's disappearance. Fled in the night to elope, ran away after making an egregious error in paperwork, got too drunk and picked a fight they couldn’t win, moved somewhere else to die of a wasting disease in an isolated sea-side cottage. It felt like adding the final highlight to a painting, to put an end to every false identity. Nyx spent so much time making these people real and believable, living their fabricated realities as her truth, as her stage makeup. It felt only right to lay the masks to rest with a proper, believable ending. 

The mountain’s bitter, magical cold avoided her gently, tasting as always Mycan’s intangible, eternal touch on her. All these years, and still Mycan’s magic lingered in her, drifting along the shoulders he would touch, reassuring, grounding her to a dead past. Lingering like a scent in fabric, like an afterimage when one stared too long into the sun, at its reflection on undisturbed snowfall. The supernatural chill would not settle into her bones as it did most others, would not try to use her survival instincts to drive her away. 

But be damned if the snow didn’t get in her boots. 

Magical cold could avoid her as much as it wanted, but it created very non-magical weather patterns, which created its own non-magical snow, its own (very real) frigid temperatures, and its own non-magical sopping wet noise when it melted in ones boots and soaked through their layers of socks. 

The journey up started in the afternoon, and had turned into a tired trudge by the time the sun was but a smudge of pink on the farthest horizon. She had reached the old stone stairs, powdery snow blown into the corners by the unquieted gusts of winds that moved her hair into her face at irregular intervals. These had been meant as their own sort of reward for making it most of the way up the mountain, a path more mindlessly travelled than the hike below, but they usually were just shelves of dusty and treacherous ice that one could have great fun fracturing their assorted bones on, if they weren’t abundantly cautious. 

The lord who had put them in had meant well, but never had been very good at thinking things through. 

The lights of the mansion were on, and Nyx paused on the landing she had reached. She stared up just for a moment, letting the timeless, hopeless fantasy pull her in to its tempting embrace. Imagining, for just a second, that she could walk in the door and find the mansion just as it had been a thousand, three thousand, five thousand years ago. That she could walk through those doors like walking back through the years. That she could walk through halls full with the scrambling children of her greatest friends and battle-bonded family, and on the other side of the house, she could look down on a town under construction, a people hand picked to create utopia, frozen in that perfect era of peace-before-reality, ambition and hope all they knew. 

The outside of the mansion still looked the same as it ever had been, when viewed from the blizzard side of things. The lanterns hanging down from the windows, so that the housekeepers could easily light and refill them, stayed bright and flickering. The house still rose like an extension of the mountain, still looked as secure and as safe. 

It was the inside where things had slowly changed. A rug wore out, and then it was gone. A vase got broken, and replaced with something new. A table was restored with the wrong stain, and had the knobs replaced with something else, and looked different. A child grew up and left. A servant retired and trained somebody new. A lord died, and their memorial portrait joined the others in a dusty, unvisited hall. 

Something was always changed, when Nyx returned from her duties. 

But those lights, that view always stayed the same to Nyx. The past did matter, just a little bit, to Nyx, who had so much of it to re-live. So much of it to mourn, if they really wanted to. But they had resolved not to mourn their age-- to pretend that they’d already died, just for committing the great sin of surviving life. It was a necessary conclusion to come to. 

Somebody was still awake in the kitchen. Nyx could see the rim of half-lidded windows burning, could see somebody’s shadow interrupt the lantern light from inside periodically in their pacing. They picked their (wet and cold) feet up, and began walking again. 

  

“And just what are you doing?” Nyx asked, leaning herself in the wide doorway. 

Moss, gangly and teenager-shaped, must have jumped half xir new height for how hard xe flinched.
  
“Trying to-- to-- establish good habits?” Xe turned and smiled, almost apologetic, as if guilty for having been caught doing something so average and base as washing dishes. 

“Bah, you were free of that burden as soon as the Stars decided to make you Guinevere’s child.” Nyx rolled their eyes, and kicked their puddle-secreting boots to rest near the room’s wood stove, where some large pot of something gathered heat on the cast-iron surface. Their several pairs of socks, not quite equally as wet, followed suit shortly thereafter. Their bag of belongings remained, out of the way and mostly forgotten, in the hallway. 

Moss flicked xir hands free of water and turned to face xir uncle properly, a real, unapologetic smile splitting xir face. 

“You’re home,” Xe observed, voice doing a dance on the edge of high and low, seeming unable to decide. It didn’t used to sound like that before Nyx left. 

“Am I?” Nyx twisted her neck to look around at the kitchen, pretending to notice it for the first time. “Ah, damn it, I suppose I am back in this hovel once again. And I suppose you must be my nephew, even though you’re twice the size xe was I left xem!” 

“Did I get taller?” Moss asked, eyebrows flicking up eagerly. “Really? It's noticeable?” 

“No, you’re the size of a mouse. What sort of question-- *Yes*, you got taller, Moss, you're practically hitting your head on things, look at you.” Nyx laughed, bringing their hands to rest over the stove to drive the numb chill from them. “You certainly don’t fit on my lap anymore.”

“Well, way to make me feel excited about it,” Moss huffed, crossing xir arms and looking away, a comical affair, now that xe looked so knobbly and awkward.  

“Anybody can get taller, and it would happen to you whether you willed it or not. Somebody has to keep you humble, lordling. I’ve been doing it to this family for generations.” 

“Our most beloved curse, truly."  

“Ah, save it. What’re you making?” Nyx lifted the cover of the pot, peaking around the puff of steam. 

“A little soup from the leftovers of last night’s dinner. Somebody in the valley had chickens up for slaughter and offered us some for a discounted price, so. . . soup.” 

“You’re spicing it adequately, I hope.” Nyx put the lid down, choosing to ignore for now that Moss and xir parents had no real need to make their culinary decisions motivated by cost effectiveness. 

“I would be foolish not to.” 

“Damn right. And you’re washing as you go,” She gestured at his sink full of water approvingly.

“I don’t like making people clean up my mess for me.” Moss mumbled. 
  
“Good.” Nyx nodded, and walked over to the cabinets, pulling out a bowl and going for the flour. 

“I try.” Moss shrugged. 

“I know. It’s easy to be a snot nosed brat at your age, though. I was, and I know plenty of your great grandparents were.” Nyx kept working, letting her bare feet rest on the warm tiles of the floor and soak up the heat. 

Moss hovered near Nyx, buzzing with unspoken words as Nyx stirred. It radiated from xem in the way that had become the norm-- the nature of things when Nyx was in and out and always casual. Not wanting to make a big deal of her arrival, in case she suddenly vanished again, in case xe would have to get used to her absence

“Where’s your mom?” Nyx asked. “Lord Mom.”  
  
“She’s in the valley. There was some sort of land dispute she’s going to settle. She said not to wait up, so I’m serving myself dinner. And you now, I guess. Mamma went with her.” 

“So it’s just us?”  
  
“And the servants, if they’re not already gone home.”

Nyx nodded. “Just like the good old days, then.” They raided the spice rack, taking a wild guess at what Moss had put in the broth and selecting what herbs would play off that. 

She knew that she was obligated to report to Kettle, and she would-- just as soon as her Lordship wasn't busy, of course. It was only polite not to interrupt her. The notebook in her backpack, her discoveries written in double-language, could wait until Kettle's return to be discussed. The biscuits, however, only had so long to be made and dropped into the soup to cook, and Nyx prided herself on her priorities. 

Moss didn't have anything to add but a quiet smile, moving to stir the soup. 

“I’m really glad you’re home," Xe spoke up, after a silence had stretched between them. 

“I’m glad I’m home too, lordling.” Nyx smiled, and she really did mean it.
