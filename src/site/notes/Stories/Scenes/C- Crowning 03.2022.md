Description: The one in which the Mountains are safe and Mycan gifts Nyx a crown

**

Nyx stood at the banister of the newly built manor, a glass of wine held loosely in their hand that dangled over the edge. They were watching the starlight, admiring the sweet promises of protection they’d heard many times beneath them. There was music playing inside, children running beneath her, laughing, throwing around little balls of faerie fire as the lights fluttered and cascaded amongst the trees. They may never see another war, if all goes well. May not even remember the last one. To live in a world of perfect safety and prosperity. She almost envied them.

“Had enough of the party?” The newly anointed High Lord stepped out, a bottle of wine armed in one hand to offer to them.

Their eyes were like lanterns, a bitter reminder that they were less than… but that was no more. Not in this world they’ve created, together, “I’ve had enough of watching you make a fool of yourself.” They laughed, offering their glass in quiet acceptance.

“I was hardly making a fool of myself.” 

“You stood on top of the table and started dancing.”

“Standard revelry.” 

“Your children are watching.”

That had shut him up, and he watched Nyx for a quiet, patient moment.

“Your drunken stupor turns you into an idiot and a fool,” She drank from her glass, watching him, “and that behavior isn’t very lordly of you.”

“No. No, I suppose it’s not.” He laughed in a low tone, setting the bottle down before summoning a ball of faelight between them. 

And Nyx, for a moment, just stared. Simply admired him, and how the light danced like fresh snow in his hair. How it flickered against his crown, a brilliant silver with small sapphires pressed in like powder. It was made of materials so rich in the mountains, so precious to his land. Enough precious metals to make generations of jewelry.

He couldn’t help but smile, as if knowing the unspoken words behind those strange eyes. And in the light, he pulled out a box, black as night and tied simply with a shimmering gold ribbon.

Her eyes narrowed, watching the box, “What-”

“You have refused to tell me when your birthday is.” He carefully lifted her free hand, placing the box into them. It was wide, far too big to be a necklace, which had been more of his style of gifts, “And today was the first successful harvest of the rest of our lives.” He laughed, taking the glass from her so she could hold the box better, “So until you decide to tell me, I couldn’t think of a better night to celebrate.”

Nyx gently shook the box. It was heavy, but not unpleasantly so, “I… don’t think I can accept this.” She tried to hand it back. How could she take away from such an important day for him, for everyone?

But he pushed the box back into her chest, smiling fondly, “At least open it before you turn it down.”

Her nose crinkled, but as well as she could hold her ground, she knew Mycan was just as stubborn. So instead of fighting, she carefully pulled at the ribbon. And when she opened the box, she sucked in a breath, looking at what lay within a bed of purple velvet. Something only ever gifted to noble blood. Something she’d never expected to be gifted to her.

“I need you to help me.” He leaned against the railing, taking the lid from her so she could better look over the contents, “I don’t want to bring up a new world following old ideals. I need someone to help colead, and show everyone that even they could have a say. I don’t want people to think they’ll simply have a monarch.”

She pulled the crown, blacker than night, only barely reflecting any light, and turning it to stardust. It formed a perfect circle, vines weaving and winding amongst each other. They looked like thorns, tiny, budding roses at its ends. It was beautiful.

“Nyx, you balance me.” He’d spoken quietly, as if in secret confession, “When I’m dreaming too high, you’re the one to keep my feet on the ground… and that’s not a bad thing!” He nervously corrected, “But you’ve always been there to keep my head on my shoulders, to keep me rational.”

“I could’ve been your advisor then.” She tried to push down her emotions, clutching the crown as if it’d soon be torn from her hands.

He simply shook his head, gently pushing the crown up toward her head, her fingers still clinging to it, “That doesn’t suit my vision.”

“But I’m not like you.” She then blurted out, voice wavering slightly, “Even if it’s a coleading, I’m not high fae. They wouldn’t want me.” 

“If anyone dare take offense to it, then there’s a perfectly good exit.” He spoke firmly, with promise. He managed to, carefully, take the crown from Nyx’s hands, placing it on her head and admiring her, “I know it’s a statement, but it’s more than that. You are no less important just because you’re different, and I want every child born in this land to learn that those differences only make them unique, and are not just another hurdle for them to triumph.”
