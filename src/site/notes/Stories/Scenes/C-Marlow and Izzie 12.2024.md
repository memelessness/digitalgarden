Characters:[[People/Pirates/Marlow\|Marlow]], [[People/Pirates/Izzie\|Izzie]]

Description: The one where Marlow becomes the surrogate father of a puppy

**

Marlow had been laying atop a dining table they’d pulled out onto the deck, playing his lute with a sweet and melancholic tune as he looked longingly up at the stars. It’d reminded him of when he was young, looking up from the comfort of gentle undersea pastures to watch ships go by. Each star reminded him of the fish, dancing along the waves as the sunlight streamed in ribbons. 

He listened as Izzie sat in the bench beside him, humming some sort of tune as she tried to find her notes on the viola. Her tail swished along the wood of the deck, finding the rhythm with ease. She had a particular knack for this sort of thing. In another life, they could’ve been minstrels together. 

Marlow never saw himself the nurturing type. For as many years as he’d been at sea, he’d been only looking out for himself. Sure, he had morals. Many people had morals they’d stand up for. He didn’t like how the wealthy flaunted their money while the poor starved in their hovels. He didn’t like when the strong felt the need to impose themselves upon the weak. He felt a particular strain when a complete stranger, a child nonetheless, begged for help as their father chased them around the city, threatening their very life. A man selfish enough to even demand money in exchange for the safety of his own daughter.

And so, against the better judgement of the crew, he found himself as the caretaker of an adolescent in his charge. He’d started with menial tasks. The shit he’d have to do anyway that would free up his time. And as she moped the vomit of old seasick sailors, he’d rest upon the banister and play his tunes. 

Eventually, she’d become interested. After how closely she’d followed him, like a puppy longing for a pack, he’d figured this was inevitable. She’d reach out for the strings, to the lute he’d taken such great care of since he was a boy, and he’d find himself jerking back. 
He’d never hear her ask again.

And then he’d start noticing more. The rest of the crew treated her better than he ever could. Whenever she was scared, there was somewhere to find comfort. When hungry, the kitchen would provide. He’s see her portions much greater, only later to discover the dinnerrolls she’d hide in her pillowcase.

And yet she still flocked to him. 

The day he offered to teach her the lute may have been one of his greatest moments. She was the most delicate when holding the instrument, her frame not quite big enough to hold it comfortably, and she smiled. Possibly for the first time since she joined the crew.

They’d continue these lessons for a few months, and Marlow has seen her become her own person. He was reminded of himself. She was passionate. Rowdy. And when she came into her magic, a power unique to herself, he’d become excited to teach her more. Something that his father had taught him long ago.

The first proper gift he’d given her was the very viola she’d been playing. It was done on a whim. The two of them were partying around the town, playing their usual back and forthe. Singing melodies, pitching lines, following through to make a cute little song they can later pair with strings. And then she stopped, staring at the windowfront of a music shop. He thought it fitting, when they walked into the shop and she held such a beautifully crafted thing. Far different than the dusty old lute he’d lent.

And so, they spent their nights together, bouncing off of one another with little refrains as she figured out how her future talent worked. And Marlow looked up to the sky, starlight glittering above as the moonlight cascades upon the sails. And he’d find himself singing. In a language long abandoned by change and time. 

Izzie had stopped her playing, looking up to Marlow in patience as she waited for him to quiet down, “What does that one mean?” 

Marlow sat up, pushing his braids behind him, “I don’t remember.”

Growing up, it’d become more important for him to learn the language of trade. While the sea provided, there were things that’d been impossible to acquire, and their people had been too proud to seek it themselves. So he’d never learned the language of his ancestors before him.

That didn’t keep him from the little phrases he knew. The little greetings that he’d give to his mother on those rare occasions he’d return to that dreaded farm; where kelp was traded for spinach and algae for barley. He’d learned to find the words that would console her in taking his gold. He’d tell her he’d gotten some dignified job, though he knows she of all people would understand his longing for the sea.

But that song. His father would sing it to him whenever he was scared. When large gar stalked their little town, threatening anything that moved. When their crops died after a mighty powerful current. When they had to walk away from their ancestral land, seeking refuge in the dry land above… When he was faced with his father’s deathbed, unsure what to do and how he will protect his family… He was Izzie’s age when his father passed. He felt there was some foul game being played in that. 

He thought about when he was younger. Just before the whale began to fall upon their village. His father had taken so much time to teach him the skills he would need for his pilgrimage. A coming of age that their people would take alone. A personal crusade into the ravine, where strange creatures lurked. He’d been taught of many things. Of magic. Of spirits. Of the importance of not letting a kill go to waste, and yet the responsibility to protect one’s self and others. 

His father spoke long of the whales. Holy creatures to their people. Massive beasts that sing their song along the horizon, and in death, fall to the ground below. Its meat feeds, and the bones become home to smaller creatures. The ocean thrives.

Marlow brought his lute back to his chest, “I can teach it to you though. If you’d like.”

Izzie’s ears tucked back, a little smile crossing over her eyes as she brought her viola to her chin. She would never pry, but she felt comfort in Marlow wanting to share something so important with her.
