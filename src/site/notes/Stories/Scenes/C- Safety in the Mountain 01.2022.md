Description: (Maybe not canon?) The one where Cosmos first climbs the Mountains

**

Moss stood straight, arms behind xir back as xe waited outside the dining room. You’ve always been such a serious kid, xir mother would always say while cupping xir cheek. But xe wasn’t the Lord of this land yet, only taking temporary control of more minor decisions. The least xe could do was show xir respect.

Xir mothers walked down the hall, arms linked together as they laughed at their quiet conversation. The shorter of the two reached out a hand, cupping Moss’s face as the words echoed softly in her eyes. And the doors opened, revealing the long table already assembled with food and cutlery.

“How was your training?” The Lord of the Mountain spoke proudly, her confidence never faltering as she pulled out a chair for her Lady. 

Moss sat xemself, already reaching out to plate some food, “Uncle Argon showed me a few weapons today.” Xe lost xir manners, speaking between bite, “Ey let me fight with some too. Said I needed to know how a good weapon feels.”

“So, xe’s working with Argon.” Gwen spoke a little bitterly, having expressed her distaste. She always said ey worked xem too hard.

Ket waited as she chewed through her food, cutting into some ham, “My brother is very skilled at what ey does.” She spoke patiently, confidence unwavering, “And I don’t trust many people with our son. Even in my own court.” 

Moss looked down to xir hands for a moment, calluses that were bleeding mere moments ago already on their way to healing much stronger. Sure, xir Uncle was tough on xem, but there wasn’t any other way to train than a little bit of tough love and labor. 

“I still think you should train xem.”

“Dearest, I promise you if I had the time, I would.”

They continued to bicker softly to each other, yet love was still held in every word. And Moss just continued to eat.

“You know you don’t have to wait on us for dinner.” Gwen decided to change the subject, her attention diverting to her son, who had been shoveling food into xir body with little care.

Moss just shrugged, reaching for a glass of water. Xe didn’t want to have this conversation right now.

Kettle straightened, her eyes focusing onto some invisible point, as if she were looking for something. Gwen watched her carefully, setting her own silverware down.

“Someone’s scaling the mountain.” She spoke calmly, as if nothing about that bothered her.

“... And what do you want to do about it?” Gwen asked in a different sort of calm, mild concern lying beneath.

The High Lord said nothing, simply folding her arms over each other, looking to their son. This was another test, xe knew that. Xe straightened up a little, wiping xir mouth with the back of xir hand as xe pushed the plate away from xem. Xe closed xir eyes, clasping xir hands together as xe tried to focus on the other side of their mountain.

It was difficult. Far more difficult than finding something in the valley. While the valley was blessed with sunshine and milder weather, the mountain itself was damned to an eternal blizzard, fuzzing xir vision like static electricity. But eventually, xe could see a shape, scraping at the snow like a rabid animal. It had to be far away from the path to need to climb.

Xe tried to get closer, tried to banish the snow from xir vision as xe tried so hard to see the face of this intruder. And then it cleared. Xe saw eyes like galaxies forced into desperation as she screamed, tears freezing, fingertips bloodied.

“I know her.” Xe kept xir eyes closed, watching her.

“Who is she?” Kettle spoke calmly. Xe couldn’t see her.

Xe looked over the girl some more. She wore nothing much outside a nightgown. She hadn’t planned to scale the mountain, “She doesn’t have a name, yet.” Xe gulped hard, brows knitting into each other, “I’ve seen her at parties. Her father is the High Lord of the Meadows.”

“So not much stronger than a flower nymph.” Her tone was careless. Xe figured xir mothers were having a silent conversation in the background, “She won’t survive the trek alone.”

Moss dug xir nails into xir knuckles, just watching. Xe hadn’t seen her in only a few years… But she was running from something. What could that be?

“If she reaches the manor, we can’t keep her from seeing the valley.” Kettle spoke with warning, “Do you know her well enough?”

“... No.” Xe admitted honestly, hating xemself for the useless response, “I’ve only ever seen her at parties. We never had any conversations outside of that.”

“But you want to save her.”

Moss felt xir gut turn as the girl seemed to be giving up. Not from her will, she was still trying, still desperate, but as her body shuddered in the cold, she was near collapsing, “I know she’s scared of the mountains… scared of us.” Scared, like most people on the outside, “She wouldn’t try without reason.”

“And you’d risk your people for that?”

Moss finally opened xir eyes, squinting to the light as xe tried to look xir mother in the eye. A fiery look of unwavering resolve, “Those who seek haven in the mountain are under our protection. We’d fail her and our ancestors if we didn’t.” And that was the decision. The first real decision xe made on par with that of a High Lord.

Kettle nodded, some sort of pride in her as she motioned her head to the dining room door, “Go get your coat.” And that was it. It was obvious then that she’d already had her resolve, merely interested if xe’d come to the same conclusion.

And xe returned the nod, slamming xir hands on the table before making a run for xir room.

“... You play such cruel games with xem.” Gwen spoke with absolute exhaustion as she stood up. That whole conversation had given her a sort of emotional whiplash. 

—

She woke up quietly at first, staring bleary eyed at the ceiling as she traced the unfamiliar lattice in her mind. Where even was she? And did she really care? 

And like a tidal wave, the memories hit her, and her breathing became erratic as she tried to move, and her body felt too weak, too weighed down.

“Hey, hey, it’s okay.” A voice spoke calmly, a hand brushing over hers as a careful reassurance.

She managed to move her head over, looking to, “... Moss?” Her voiced rasped as she clung to xir hand, desperate for some sort of reassurance. She made it. She actually made it up the mountain. Made it away from-

“Oh.” Xe spoke without thinking, snapping xir fingers toward a mug on the nightstand. Xir fingers sparkled with tiny stars, falling toward the ground and disappearing like embers. Her grip tightened a little more. “Cocoa.” He ran a thumb against the back of her hand, motioning to the mug.

She struggled to sit up, Moss helping her as best as xe could. She looked over herself, covered in furs and warmer clothing. She then looked to the nightstand, her old clothes folded beside the mug.

“Sorry.” Moss apologized, embarrassed, “We had to change your clothes. You would’ve gotten sick.”

She nodded slowly, letting go of xem to hold the mug between her hands, just staring at the little star-shaped marshmallows that floated on top, as if they’d been freshly placed in.

Moss sat back down in xir chair, the motion not going unnoticed. Xe decided to wait in that room for her to wake up. Didn’t want to leave her alone.

“... Thank you.” She spoke weakly, tears brimming her eyes as she returned to the warmth between her fingertips.

“Just worry about getting better.” Xe spoke softly, watching her carefully. Patient as she continued crying, the tears falling into the cocoa, “... Would you like me to leave?” But she shook her head in response, shuddering, “... Okay.” Xe scoot xir seat closer, about to place a hand to her arm but stopped, just keeping it close.

“... You saw, huh.” She muttered, gently reaching a hand to where the burn was and recoiling.

“It looks like magic.” Xe hesitated to speak, “It’s minor enough… but it’s taking it’s time to heal.” Xe wanted to say that she was lucky it wasn’t much worse.. But that didn’t feel like an appropriate conversation for now. “My mother sent someone to check on the meadows, but you’re safe with us. No one will ever know you’re here.” Xe was careful with every movement, gently placing warm fingers over hers, “She’s going to want to question you, when you’re ready.”

She nodded, swatting xir hand away before wiping the tears from her eyes, looking outside the window. There was snow out there. So much snow. And yet it never piled up against the windows, “... I don’t want to.”

“When you’re ready.” Xe repeated, folding xir arms over each other, “If you need centuries, we’ll give them to you.”
