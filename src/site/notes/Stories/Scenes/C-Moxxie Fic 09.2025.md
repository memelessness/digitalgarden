Soft jazz played over the radio in Moxxie’s workshop, the gentle piano guiding its hand as it worked on the latest project. 

A robotic arm, or rather it was just servicing one it’d made years ago. Time had begun taking a toll on the metal. The joints had more difficulties now, the wires fraying, the metal all but rotting. Yet despite everything, no matter how strong the electrical impulse, Moxxie couldn’t get the hand to clench properly.

It was now pulling at the cables that lay deeper in the recesses of the plating. Many had been far too old. Well overused to be operable. Some were already exposed to the elements, the blue-green corrosion overtaking it. 

Moxxie heard the footsteps that stopped at its door. While it wouldn’t call them careful, it knew it was the gentlest their owner could manage.

“You staring at me isn’t going to make the work go any faster.” It grumbled, feathers puffing up in its frustration with this gadget. Always, it was just one more problem.

“Not why I’m here, Moxx.” The voice was low. Jovial in nature as they approached Moxxie’s bench, setting a mug of coffee beside its hand.

It looked up to the fire genasi with a tired smile, setting down its tools to hold the mug in its hands. The coffee was still warm, heating its aching fingers through all the torment it’s faced in the last few years. It brought the drink to its face, taking a deep breath in as waves of peppermint and chocolate hit its senses. Still sweet yet mellowed, and the perfect amount of cream to help it go down easy.

Moxxie let out a low, slow sigh as it brought the mug back down to the workbench, carefully tapping its fingers to the ceramic, “Thanks.” It spoke softly, looking back up to its business associate.

Tenor had already been busying himself, reaching into the breast pocket of his shirt to grab a box. Without second thought, he opened the box and held it out to Moxxie, pushing a cigarette to the front.

As if by second nature, Moxxie reached out a hand, gingerly taking the offer and placing the cigarette in its mouth. It leaned forward, hand placed firmly between the two, tailfeathers gently swishing against the bench.

And the genasi was more than happy to oblige, closing some of the distance as he reached toward its face. He snapped his fingers purposefully, a flame igniting at the tip of his thumb, and he held it there.

Moxxie took in a deep inhale before pulling back, leaning away from its companion as it turned to face the window. Ribbons of smoke escaped from its mouth; a merciful quiet they weren’t afraid to share.

It watched out the window for a still moment, watching how the citylights shone against the darkened sky. The Sun had already set? How long ago had that happened? Another day gone. Another passed where it could’ve done something with itself. Another lost in the endless amount of days it’d just let escape, left behind in a studio of lamplight and blunders.

Moxxie took another drag, this one much smaller and more intentional before it brought the cigarette away from its lips, passing it over to the genasi.

“You want to talk about it?” Tenor took the smoke from its fingers, leaning back as he brought it to his mouth. This was the tradition they’d followed. Once fallen under the excuse of making the box last has now become something so unusually intimate. Transferring from mouth to hand; to hand to mouth. A deliberate proclamation they’d made over and over again without ever putting the words out into the air. 

Moxxie clicked its tongue as the frustration was starting to bubble up again. It poured its thoughts out on this new project it’d agreed to. How all the improvements it made were done in delicate detail. How nothing was working. How it only had a few more tests before the damn thing might as well just be scrap! … But… It was good with scrap. It could still make scrap work. It just needed the time. 

And Tenor listened to it all, holding his head up as he watched Moxxie ramble on. It was a passionate fire that even he couldn’t replicate. Moxxie was someone who burned brightly when it came to its work. It held a sense of pride that he couldn’t help but behold.

“Worst case, you make a new one.” He offered, only to be met with a furrowed brow, “I can drive us up the Coast to look at some scrap in the junkyard. And if we don’t find anything, I can swing it to get the other parts down at the market.”

Moxxie pouted for a moment at the thought. This was a possibility. The worst part about working with machinery is that one day it will all become obsolete. And the very thought made it writhe, “The Mountains have metals I could work with.” It grumbled into its coffee, slouching.

Tenor sighed, taking the cigarette from his mouth and passing it over, “The Mountains aren’t easy to barter with.” He dropped his hand as the smoke left his fingers, “And no one who’s climbed it has ever made it back.”

But that wasn’t true. Guinevere made it back. Many times in fact. And if Moxxie had realized in time, it could’ve gotten that information from her somehow. She had a natural affection for her warlocks… Madryck could’ve sweet talked her into it…

Moxxie leaned back as it carded a hand through its crown feathers, “No, no you’re right.” Smoke cascaded from its mouth as it spoke, curling around its nose in delicate whisps. Moxxie looked over, Tenor's eyes following its every move, “I… Wouldn’t make you pay for the parts though. Consider it part of the warranty.”

The genasi couldn’t help but laugh, boisterous and sweet, a melody the small bird could reminisce in, “I’m not too sure that’s how that works, Moxx.”

It lifted its nose with a stern sniff, an air of haughtyness following it, “Well, my work is held to a much higher standard! Built to last guar-an-teed.” This wasn’t a lie. Not fully, anyway. Sure, the expectations it had were high, but it knew when to be reasonable.

Tenor reached over it to take hold of the cigarette once more, a tenuous breach to their regular routine, “Well then, I have no doubt the next will be even better.”

Moxxie shrunk a bit to the closeness, grinning sheepishly in its excitement as it looked over the genasi’s features. He’d been aging a bit too… his eyes sunk heavier, the edges of his hair graying. The Flats had no means of providing, and being so far away from magic… It was enough to even drive some people mad.

Its smile faltered as it reached a hand to its own face, its glasses lifting as it traced over the deepening wrinkles in its eyes.

It watched him. Briefly. The streaks of smoke going past his beard, intermingling with the delicate glow he gave, “You… don’t have to stay here, you know.” Moxxie broke the silence, busying its hands in the sleeves of its shirt.

“Yeah?” He spoke in hushed tones, tucking a finger under Moxxie’s chin so they could meet eyes, “And where else would I find a better mechanic?”

Moxxie couldn’t help the tender laughter that escaped it, reaching a hand to hold his, “Well-”

Moxxie’s train of thought was tormented as the radio began to buzz, a rushing static overtaking the music. They’d whispered a familiar chant to Moxxie. Taunting it. Keeping their secrets under a tightly-wrapped veil, taped together with duct tape and smothered in superglue. 

Tenor tried to be smooth about it. Tried to reach his hand over to the radio to turn down the droning. Just to bring Moxxie back to him, if only for a moment. Only to be returned with it smacking his hand out of the way.

Moxxie faced a brief moment of clarity in this. What had it done? It’d never… It didn’t want to hurt Tenor like this. And yet, it pulled the radio closer, turning the volume up so only static filled the room.

“Leave me.” It spoke demandingly, its eyes not leaving the radio, its body unwavering. 

Tenor knew. Nothing would pull it from its need to listen to the static all the way through. With a groan, he stood back up, looking to Moxxie once more, somewhat hopeful it’ll snap out of it.

“... Make sure you get some sleep.” He muttered, setting the cigarette next to Moxxie. The embers shook off on the impact, cascading downward, fizzling out before it could reach the floor, “I can be ready to go as early as morning.”

Moxxie listened to his heavy footsteps as he left, reaching out to its coffee to take another sip. Already, the mug had lost heat, its fingers aching a bit upon the sudden realization.

It picked up the cigarette as it looked over its shoulder, watching the door of its workshop close with a quiet creak. It took a deep breath, returning to the radio as it dropped the smoke into its mug and set it aside. 

Moxxie knew it didn’t deserve such gentleness. It knew it seemed deranged… and yet, it did this anyway. It wasn’t going to change.
